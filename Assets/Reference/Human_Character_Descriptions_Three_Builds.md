# THE SHATTERED DOMINION — Human Race Character Descriptions
## Three Fully Equipped Builds | Image Generation Reference
**Visual Register:** Register 1 (Ordinary World) — grounded realism, photorealistic textures, weathered and lived-in
**Art Direction Anchor:** The Witcher 3, Crimson Desert, Skyrim — nothing looks like a fantasy illustration. These are people who live in armor, sleep in dirt, and carry what they need.

## CHARACTER 1: THE LATTICE WARDEN (Light Armor / Mage / Staff)

**Origin Implied:** Relic Hunter — a scholar who crawled out of a collapsing Firstborn ruin and learned to weaponize what nearly killed them.

### Overall Silhouette & Posture
A tall, narrow figure — approximately 1.80m — draped in layered robes that fall nearly to the ankle, creating a long, vertical silhouette that reads immediately as someone who wields knowledge rather than brute force. Not frail — there is lean, functional strength beneath the fabric, the kind built from years of climbing through ruins and hauling equipment — but the robes obscure it. The posture is upright and still, weight centered, chin slightly lifted with the particular composure of someone accustomed to concentrating under pressure. The hood is pulled up, casting the upper face in shadow. The staff is held in the right hand, planted loosely on the ground beside the body, its crystalline headpiece rising above the character's shoulder — an unmistakable visual anchor that identifies this figure as a caster before anything else registers. The left hand hangs at the side, fingers slightly spread, relaxed but ready.

### Head & Face
Mostly obscured by the hood. What is visible: the lower half of a sharp-featured face — angular jaw, thin lips slightly parted, weathered olive-brown skin showing sun damage and the rough texture of someone who has spent years in places where the air is dry and mineral. A faded scar runs diagonally from the left cheekbone down to the jaw — old, healed clean, from ruin debris. Stubble, several days old. The chin and mouth express quiet focus. Above, the hood casts the eyes into deep shadow — in certain light, the faint pale blue-white reflection of the Relic Mark's glow is barely visible in the darkness beneath the hood, catching in the eyes like distant starlight. Hair is not visible. The hood conceals everything above the cheekbones.

**Head Armor — Mage's Cowl:** A deep, voluminous hood of heavy arcane-woven cloth, currently raised and pulled forward to shadow the face. The cowl is a dark charcoal gray, made from a tightly woven wool-and-hemp blend that has been treated against moisture, with the fabric noticeably heavier and stiffer than ordinary cloth — the arcane weave gives it a denser hand. The interior lining is visible where the hood drapes back at the sides of the face: a slightly lighter gray threaded with faint blue-white geometric patterns — not decorative embroidery, but functional lattice-conductive weave that improves spell channeling when the hood is raised. The patterns are subtle, almost invisible in flat light, but catch and glimmer faintly in overcast shadow like threads of moonlight woven into the fabric. The cowl integrates with the robe at the collar — it is not a separate garment but an extension of the robe's upper construction, rising from a high collar that covers the neck completely. Two small tarnished brass clasps at the throat secure the collar closed. The fabric of the hood shows wear — a small mend near the left clasp where the cloth tore and was stitched with mismatched thread, and a faint discoloration along the brow where years of sweat and rain have darkened the edge.

### Torso
**Chest Armor — Arcanist's Robe (Light):** A full-length robe falling to approximately 10cm above the ankle — long enough to drape and move with the body, short enough to not drag in mud. This is the character's defining visual element. The robe is constructed in two layers. The outer layer is a dense, heavy-weight woven fabric in a deep desaturated indigo-black — a color that once had richness to it but has been dulled and shifted by years of use, weather, and the particular chemical staining that comes from proximity to lattice energy. The fabric has a subtle texture — not smooth like silk but not rough like burlap — a tight, almost felted weave with visible body. The inner layer, glimpsed where the robe parts during movement, is a lighter fabric woven with the same blue-white geometric arcane threading visible in the cowl — this inner lining is the robe's functional core, a lattice-conductive textile that amplifies spell energy when worn against the body.

The robe's cut is scholarly but practical. A high collar rises to the base of the jaw, merging with the cowl above. The front closes with a series of tarnished brass clasps — eight in total, running from the throat to the waist in a straight vertical line, each clasp a small hook-and-eye of age-darkened metal. Below the waist, the robe falls open in a wide front split, revealing the trousers and boots beneath and allowing full stride. The sleeves are long and moderately wide — not the exaggerated bell sleeves of fantasy illustration, but wider than a coat's sleeves, falling to the wrist with enough room to push back to the elbow when working. The cuffs are reinforced with a band of darker fabric, and the right cuff shows a faint purple-gray chemical stain from reagent handling. Across the chest and shoulders, the robe has subtle structural reinforcement — not armor, but a heavier weave at stress points that gives the upper body a slightly more defined shape beneath the drape.

The robe's surface tells its history: a mended tear on the right side of the chest, repaired with a slightly darker fabric patch; the left elbow re-stitched; the hem darkened and stiffened where it has repeatedly dragged through puddles and been dried over campfires; and a specific area on the back, between the shoulder blades, that is slightly faded — the spot where a pack has rested during years of travel.

**Belt — Leather Belt (Light):** A simple dark brown leather belt, medium width, worn at the natural waist over the robe — cinching the fabric and defining the silhouette. The leather is thick but supple from regular oiling. Three visible attachments only: a leather pouch on the left hip, stained faintly purple-gray from Corruption zone reagents; a small leather cradle on the right hip holding a relic scanner — a palm-sized angular Firstborn device with a bronze-undertone metallic surface covered in tiny functional rune-script; and a loop behind the right hip from which the Catalyst Orb hangs in its cradle when not in use. The belt buckle is plain iron, pitted with age. The belt is functional, not decorative — it exists to carry essentials and to keep the robe from billowing during spellwork.

**Shoulders — Cloak (Unarmored):** A long traveling cloak falling to mid-calf, fastened at the throat with a simple iron brooch pin. The cloak is a muted dark green-brown — the color of deep forest canopy in autumn — made from heavy felted wool that repels rain. It is worn over the robe, adding another layer to the character's draped silhouette. In the default pose, the cloak is thrown back over the left shoulder, revealing the robe beneath on the left side while draping over the right shoulder and arm. When the character is walking or standing in wind, the cloak and robe move together in overlapping layers — a visual depth that reads as weight and fabric, not as costume. The cloak's edge is raw-cut, not hemmed. One corner near the bottom is visibly singed, the wool blackened and stiffened from a past encounter with something hot. The brooch pin at the throat is simple hammered iron, but the clasp itself — barely visible — bears a tiny etched mark: a Firstborn rune-script symbol. Whether this is decoration or function is ambiguous.

### Arms & Hands
**Hands — Runecarver's Gloves (Unarmored):** Thin, close-fitting gloves of supple dark leather, extending to mid-forearm. The leather is exceptionally fine — this is the one piece of equipment where quality was prioritized, because the hands are the instrument. The fingertips are cut away on the index and middle fingers of both hands, exposing bare skin for precision work with relic interfaces and rune inscriptions. The exposed fingers show calluses, small burn scars, and the faint pale blue-white trace of the Relic Mark running along the inner right forearm — visible as a subtle geometric pattern beneath the skin that pulses very faintly, barely perceptible in daylight. The gloves are secured at the forearm with three small leather straps and iron buckles. Around the left wrist, over the glove, is a simple cord bracelet with two small Firstborn-stone beads — angular, blue-gray, with the faint iridescence of engineered material. This is not decoration. It is a calibration aid for relic work.

### Legs & Feet
**Legs — Traveler's Trousers (Unarmored):** Heavy canvas trousers in a dark gray-brown, tucked into the boots. Reinforced at the knees with additional patches of leather — a practical addition for someone who spends time kneeling in ruins. The trousers are loose enough for full range of movement but not baggy. A leather thigh strap on the right leg holds a sheathed dagger — a simple dirk with a leather-wrapped handle, positioned for quick draw.

**Feet — Leather Boots:** Calf-height boots in dark brown leather, well-worn. The soles are thick and scored from walking on Firstborn stone. The leather has a specific patina — darker where water has repeatedly soaked in, lighter where it has dried and flexed. Laced up the front with leather cord, the lacing replaced at the top eyelets with a different-colored cord (darker, newer — a field repair). The boots are functional frontier footwear, not military issue.

### Weapon — Quarterstaff (Two-Hand Focus)
A staff approximately 1.9m in length — taller than the wielder — made from a dense, dark ironwood shaft that serves as the foundation for something more complex. The lower two-thirds of the staff is the shaft proper: dark wood, smooth from years of handling, its surface darkened by palm oil and use to a near-black finish. A leather cord wrap at the midpoint provides the primary grip. The wood follows a natural subtle curve that the maker preserved, giving the staff an organic line. The base is capped with an iron ferrule, scored from contact with stone.

The upper third is where the staff becomes unmistakably a mage's weapon. Beginning approximately 60cm from the top, the wood is inlaid with a spiraling channel of Firstborn-stone alloy — a bronze-undertone metal conduit that wraps the shaft in a helical pattern, its surface inscribed with tiny angular rune-script. The channel is not decorative — it is a lattice energy conduit, and the rune-script is functional calibration marking. The helix tightens as it rises, the turns growing closer together until, at the top of the shaft, the wood terminates and the metal conduit opens into a setting that cradles the staff's headpiece: a Firstborn crystal approximately 12cm long and 6cm wide, a faceted, angular shard of engineered stone with a translucent, smoky blue-gray quality. The crystal is not a perfect geometric shape — it has the precise-but-alien geometry of Firstborn design, with flat planes meeting at angles that feel slightly wrong to the eye, as if the stone was cut according to mathematics that humans do not use. In daylight, the crystal is beautiful but inert — a striking piece of mineral held in a metal cradle at the top of a dark staff. In dim light or during casting, the crystal's interior reveals faint geometric lattice structures suspended within the stone — not carved, but existing within the material like inclusions in amber — and the pale blue-white glow of active lattice energy travels up through the helical conduit channels from the grip to the crystal, illuminating the rune-script along the way in a visible pulse that takes approximately one second to travel the full length. This glow is restrained — not a beacon, but a visible sign of the energy moving through the weapon. It is the same pale blue-white as Firstborn relic energy, as corruption accents, as the lattice itself. When not casting, the conduit channels and crystal are dormant — the staff reads as an unusual but static object, clearly crafted with purpose, its nature apparent to anyone who has seen Firstborn technology.

**Offhand — Catalyst Orb (when not two-handing the staff):** A sphere approximately 8cm in diameter, made from the same smoky, translucent Firstborn crystal material as the staff's headpiece. Inside the sphere, faint geometric lattice patterns are visible — not etched on the surface, but existing within the material itself. The sphere sits in a small leather cradle attached to the belt when not in use, and is held in the left hand when the character casts one-handed. The orb and the staff headpiece are visually related — clearly from the same material tradition — creating a unified arcane aesthetic across the character's equipment.

### Worn Details & Storytelling Elements
The overall impression is a figure defined by draped layers and a single striking vertical element — the staff with its crystalline headpiece rising above the hooded silhouette. The color palette is dark and muted: charcoal, indigo-black, dark brown, forest green-brown — with the visual interest concentrated in two areas: the Firstborn crystal and conduit work on the staff's upper third, and the faint blue-white geometric threading visible in the cowl and robe lining where the fabric parts or catches light. The Relic Mark on the inner right forearm provides a third point of arcane visual connection — the same pale blue-white, linking the character's body to their weapon to the lattice itself. The robes, the hood, the staff, and the crystal all work together to create a silhouette that reads unmistakably as a mage — not a flashy fantasy wizard, but a person who has spent years learning to channel forces that predate human civilization, and who wears the evidence of that study in every layer of worn, arcane-threaded fabric.

## CHARACTER 2: THE VALE STALKER (Medium Armor / Archer / Hooded)

**Origin Implied:** Frontier Settler — someone who grew up hunting the forests and fields of the Severant Vale, and whose survival skills translated directly into a deadlier purpose when the Severance turned the landscape hostile.

### Overall Silhouette & Posture
A medium-height figure, approximately 1.73m, with a compact and athletic build — not bulky, but visibly strong through the shoulders and back from years of drawing a bow and carrying game. The posture is still and balanced, weight evenly distributed, with the particular economy of movement that comes from a life spent not startling prey. The hood is up, casting the upper face in shadow. The bow is held low in the left hand, arrow nocked but not drawn — ready, not aggressive. The quiver sits on the right hip, angled forward for a smooth draw. Every element of the figure reads as someone who is comfortable being unseen.

### Head & Face
Beneath the hood, a face shaped by outdoor living. Broad cheekbones, strong jaw, skin that is tanned and roughened by wind and weather — a medium-brown complexion darkened further across the nose and forehead by sun exposure. Pale hazel eyes that catch light in a way that makes them slightly unsettling in shadow. A thin scar crosses the bridge of the nose — something fast and sharp, probably an animal. The hair is cropped close on the sides and longer on top, a practical cut that keeps it out of the eyes and prevents anything from grabbing it. What is visible is dark brown, lightly matted from being under the hood.

**Head Armor — Ranger's Hood (Light):** A deep hood made from a medium-weight oiled leather, dyed to a dark forest green-brown that blends naturally with the Vale's autumn palette. The hood is not a separate garment — it is integrated into the shoulder armor, emerging from the collar of the leather chest piece and extending up and over the head as a single continuous piece. The interior is lined with a soft, dark wool for warmth and sound dampening. When raised, the hood extends far enough forward to cast the upper face into deep shadow while leaving the mouth and chin visible — the wearer can speak, breathe, and sight a bow without obstruction. At the hood's peak, the leather is double-layered and slightly stiffened to maintain its shape against rain and wind. Two small ventilation slits, barely visible, are cut near the temples. Around the hood's opening, the leather edge is raw-cut and has darkened unevenly from rain exposure — the top is nearly black, the sides still showing the original dye color. A leather chin strap hangs loose on the left side, unused — the hood stays in place through fit, not fastening.

### Torso
**Chest Armor — Leather Armor (Medium):** A close-fitting torso piece built for mobility above all else. The core is a hardened breastplate of boiled leather, shaped precisely to the torso — not loose, not bulky, but fitted tight enough that the body's movement translates directly through the armor without lag or shift. The color is a deep, rich umber brown with darker staining where sweat and rain have been absorbed over years. The breastplate is constructed from four main panels: two front panels overlapping at the centerline and two side panels wrapping under the arms, joined with fine waxed-cord stitching and minimal iron rivets — small, flat-headed, only where structurally necessary. The armor is cut high at the waist, ending just below the ribs rather than extending to the belly — this sacrifices lower torso protection in exchange for complete freedom of the hips and waist, allowing the wearer to twist, crouch, and draw a bow without any restriction. Below the breastplate, only a thin layer of soft leather extends over the abdomen — flexible, form-fitting, offering minimal protection but zero interference with movement. No chainmail skirt, no studded panels beneath. The silhouette is lean: armor that follows the body's lines rather than adding bulk. On the right side, near the lower ribs, a section has been replaced with a slightly different-colored leather — damage repaired with what was available. The overall impression is protection for the vitals and nothing more — the rest is speed.

**Belt — Leather Belt (Light):** A narrow, close-fitting belt of single-layer leather with a flat iron buckle, worn snug over the armor at the natural waist. The belt is deliberately minimal — nothing hangs from it that would swing, catch, or make noise. Two attachments only: the quiver mount on the right hip, holding the main quiver at an angle for a fast draw, and a single small pouch on the left hip — flat against the body, containing a spare bowstring and a tin of wax. Nothing else. No rope, no tools, no utility gear. Everything this character carries is either worn tight against the body or left behind. The belt shows a specific pattern of wear where the quiver mount has pressed into the leather daily, but the overall impression is clean and stripped-down — a person who has decided exactly what they need and discarded everything else.

**Shoulders — Cloak (Unarmored) with minimal Leather Guard:** No pauldrons. Instead, a short half-cloak of oiled dark green-brown wool hangs from the right shoulder, falling to elbow length on the right side — enough to break up the silhouette and obscure the shoulder line without adding bulk or restricting the bow arm. The cloak is fastened with a single leather toggle at the right collarbone. On the left shoulder (bow arm), only a thin leather guard is present — a flat, close-fitting piece approximately 8cm wide that sits flush against the shoulder cap, preventing the bowstring from catching on fabric during a draw. This guard is stitched directly into the chest armor's shoulder seam, adding no visible profile. The asymmetry — cloaked right side, bare left side — is functional: the cloak shields the quiver and drawing arm from rain while leaving the bow arm completely unencumbered. In motion, the half-cloak moves with the body, and its dark color against the dark armor makes the figure's outline harder to read at distance.

### Arms & Hands
**Forearms — Leather Bracers:** Close-fitting bracers of hardened leather, extending from wrist to mid-forearm. The left bracer (bow arm) is the more heavily constructed of the two — an archer's bracer with a flat, smooth inner surface to prevent bowstring catch, and a reinforced outer surface for general protection. The right bracer is thinner, optimized for dexterity — the hand that draws the string needs fine motor control. Both are secured with two buckled straps each. The left bracer shows the specific polished wear pattern of regular bowstring contact — a smooth, slightly concave track along the inner surface.

**Hands — Archer's Gloves (Medium):** Form-fitting leather gloves with reinforced fingertips on the right hand — a three-finger shooting tab integrated into the glove design, the leather doubled over the index, middle, and ring fingers where the string contacts. The left hand glove is simpler, providing grip and protection without the shooting reinforcement. The leather is dark, supple, well-oiled. The gloves extend to the wrist, where they tuck under the bracers. The right glove shows significant wear at the string-contact points — the leather is compressed and polished smooth.

### Legs & Feet
**Legs — Leather Leggings (Medium):** Close-fitting trousers of cured leather over a wool underlayer — slim-cut, almost like a second skin. The leather is a slightly lighter brown than the torso armor, suggesting these are newer or from a different source. The inner thighs are reinforced with thin suede patches for grip when crouching or climbing. The knees have minimal articulated leather panels — two overlapping scales per knee, kept small and tight to maintain the leg's clean line while flexing with movement. No thigh straps, no visible weapons on the legs. The silhouette from the waist down is unbroken and narrow — nothing protrudes, nothing swings, nothing catches. A single dirk is sheathed horizontally at the small of the back, handle to the right, tucked behind the belt — the only backup weapon, positioned where it adds no profile to the body's outline.

**Feet — Soft-Soled Hunting Boots:** Low-cut boots in dark leather, reaching just above the ankle. The defining characteristic is the sole — thinner and softer than standard boots, made from layered leather rather than heavy cowhide or wood. These are tracking boots, designed to feel the ground beneath them and to move silently. The trade-off is visible: less protection, less insulation, but the wearer can walk on dried leaves without a sound. The boots are laced tight with waxed cord. The leather shows the kind of deep, saturated staining that comes from walking through damp undergrowth daily.

### Weapons
**Main Hand — Recurve Bow:** A recurve bow approximately 130cm tip to tip when strung. The limbs are a composite of layered wood and horn — dark at the core (yew or similar dense wood), lighter at the belly where horn lamination is visible as a slightly amber, translucent material. The limbs curve forward at the tips in the characteristic recurve shape, with horn nocks at each end for the bowstring. The grip section is wrapped in dark leather, slightly thicker on the bow hand side. The bowstring is a tightly braided linen cord, waxed until it has a slight sheen. The bow is not decorated — it is a working weapon, maintained with obsessive care. The wood surface is oiled and shows the particular glow of regularly maintained composite material. One limb has a very faint, hairline crack visible near the tip — still functional, but the bow is approaching the end of its service life, and the archer knows it.

**Quiver & Arrows:** A hip-mounted quiver of hardened leather, dark brown-black, positioned on the right hip and angled forward at approximately 30 degrees from vertical. The quiver holds approximately 20 arrows, fletched with gray goose feathers trimmed low and tight for minimal wind resistance. The shafts are ash wood, straight and uniform. Three arrows near the front of the quiver have different fletching — darker feathers, slightly longer — these are broadheads for larger targets, distinguishable by touch. The quiver itself is unadorned except for functional elements: a leather flap that can be secured over the top in rain, a drainage hole at the bottom, and a small tool pouch stitched to the side containing a bowstring wax tin and spare nock points.

### Worn Details & Storytelling Elements
The color palette is autumn forest: deep umber brown, forest green-brown, dark leather. Nothing catches light unnecessarily — iron hardware is minimal and dulled with use. The character is dressed to disappear. The silhouette is narrow, tight, with no loose elements that would catch wind or make noise. The half-cloak breaks the shoulder line; the hood obscures the face; the armor hugs the body without adding bulk. Every piece of equipment is either worn flush against the body or absent entirely. The Relic Mark is not visible — it is on the chest, beneath the breastplate and underlayer, known only to the character. The overall impression is someone built for stealth and precision — a figure you might not notice in the treeline until the arrow was already in the air. Not a warrior. Not a soldier. A hunter who has simply changed what they hunt.

## CHARACTER 3: THE IRONBOUND VANGUARD (Heavy Armor / Sword & Shield)

**Origin Implied:** Frontier Settler who enlisted with the Ironbound Vanguard during the crisis — someone who lost everything in the Severance and channeled that loss into the only institution still offering structure and purpose. The armor is military issue. The scars beneath it are personal.

### Overall Silhouette & Posture
A tall, imposing figure standing approximately 1.85m, with the proportions of a man who was lean before the armor and has become formidable beneath it. This is not a stocky, wide-bodied frame — the build is athletic and upright, with broad shoulders tapering to a narrower waist, long legs, and the erect posture of someone trained to carry plate without hunching into it. The armor adds mass evenly, creating a figure that reads as powerful without appearing compressed or barrel-chested. The stance is balanced and squared, feet shoulder-width apart, weight distributed with the quiet confidence of someone who has fought in this armor long enough that it feels like a second body. The shield rests on the left arm at a slight angle, its bottom edge near the left thigh — not raised, but ready. The sword is sheathed at the left hip. The overall impression is authority made physical — a warrior who looks like the kind of person an entire line of soldiers would form behind.

### Head & Face
The face is entirely concealed by the helm. What exists beneath: a square-jawed face with heavy brows and a broad nose, broken once and healed slightly crooked. Fair, ruddy skin weathered to a rough reddish tan. Blue-gray eyes. A recent scar running from the right corner of the mouth across the cheek. A short beard. Dark blond hair cut for helm-wear. None of this is visible. The helm is down. The person inside is anonymous — a wall of steel with eyes behind a visor slit.

**Head Armor — Plated Greathelm (Heavy):** A fully enclosed helm of reinforced steel that covers the entire head and face, sitting over a chainmail coif beneath. The helm's form is a tall, slightly tapered dome — rounded at the crown, narrowing subtly toward the chin, with a flat base that rests on the shoulders and upper chest via the coif and a padded leather liner inside. The face is covered by a shaped visor plate that extends from brow to chin. The visor features a narrow horizontal eye slit — approximately 8cm wide and 1.5cm tall — providing a restricted but functional field of view. Below the eye slit, the visor is pierced with a cluster of small circular breathing holes arranged in a functional pattern on the right side of the face — not symmetrical, not decorative, placed where airflow is most needed. The visor is attached to the helm by two pivot pins at the temples, allowing it to be raised for visibility when not in combat, though in the default pose it is down and locked.

The helm's surface is where the armor's ornamental character begins. The steel is the same blue-gray as the rest of the armor, but here the metalwork shows deliberate craftsmanship beyond mere function. A raised central ridge runs from the brow over the crown to the nape — structural reinforcement that has been worked into a subtle decorative crest, the ridge slightly wider and more pronounced than strictly necessary, with a faintly beveled edge that catches light. On either side of the ridge, shallow etched lines follow the curvature of the dome in sweeping, symmetrical arcs — not heraldic imagery, not filigree, but a restrained pattern of linear scrollwork that suggests the hand of a skilled smith who took pride in the work. The etching is shallow enough that it would be invisible at distance and visible only in raking light up close — a mark of quality, not of ostentation. Around the brow band, a row of small iron rivets — twelve in total — secures the visor mechanism and reinforces the lower edge, each rivet slightly domed and evenly spaced. The overall effect is a helm that reads as both formidable and crafted — military equipment made by someone who understood that the best armor inspires confidence in the wearer as well as fear in the opponent.

Beneath the helm, a chainmail coif of fine-linked riveted iron rings covers the head, neck, and upper shoulders — the mail draping to just above the collarbone, its lower edge concealed beneath the gorget of the chest armor. The coif provides the layer of flexible protection that fills the gaps the rigid helm cannot cover.

### Torso
**Chest Armor — Half-Plate over Chainmail Hauberk (Heavy):** This is the most visually complex element of the character. The foundation is a full chainmail hauberk — a knee-length shirt of interlinked iron rings that covers the torso, upper arms to the elbow, and extends as a skirt to mid-thigh. The mail is visible at the upper arms, under the armpits, at the sides of the torso where the plate doesn't cover, and as a skirt beneath the plate waist. The color is iron-gray with the specific subtle shimmer of well-maintained mail — each ring catching light slightly differently, creating a granular texture.

Over the hauberk, a Full Plate Cuirass provides the primary protection — this is the character's most striking visual element. The cuirass consists of a front breastplate and back plate connected at the shoulders by hinged steel plates and at the sides by leather straps with iron buckles. The breastplate is shaped to the torso with a pronounced central ridge running vertically from the high collar to the waist — a structural spine that deflects blows away from center mass and also serves as the armor's primary decorative line. The ridge is flanked by shallow, sweeping linear engravings that follow the natural contour of the chest — the same restrained scrollwork language visible on the helm, executed in clean, symmetrical arcs that suggest flowing movement frozen in steel. These engravings are not deep or flashy — they catch light as faint lines in the steel's surface, visible primarily in raking light and shadow, giving the breastplate a quality of deliberate craftsmanship without crossing into ostentation.

The steel is thick, heavy, and finished to the same blue-gray tone as the helm — not a mirror polish, but a consistent, well-maintained surface with the faint granular texture of hand-forged steel. A shallow dent near the left pectoral has been hammered mostly flat but remains visible — evidence of an impact the armor absorbed. Around the collar, a raised gorget of articulated plates protects the throat and upper chest, its upper edge rising to meet the chainmail coif — the transition from plate to mail is clean and deliberate. Each articulated plate in the gorget carries a fine etched border line, continuing the ornamental language.

The lower edge of the breastplate extends into a fauld of four overlapping horizontal lames (articulated plates) protecting the lower abdomen and hips, each lame slightly wider than the one above, creating a flared skirt of steel that allows the wearer to bend, twist, and mount a horse. The lames show the most wear — edges rounded, surfaces scratched from contact with the sword belt — but even here, the faint border etching is present on each plate's upper edge, maintaining the armor's visual unity.

The Vanguard heraldry — an iron gauntlet on a blue-gray field — is worked into the breastplate above the right breast as an embossed relief approximately 6cm across, raised slightly from the steel surface rather than merely stamped. It is present but not dominant — an institutional mark executed with the same quality as the rest of the engraving. Beneath the heraldry, a small etched rank insignia marks the wearer as an enlisted veteran.

**Belt — Iron-Link Belt (Heavy):** A belt constructed from interlocking iron plates — each plate approximately 3cm wide, hinged to the next, forming a flexible metal band. The belt sits over the fauld, securing the plate to the body and distributing the weight of the sword and accessories. The sword scabbard hangs from the left side on two leather straps. A small leather pouch on the right rear holds a whetstone and oiling cloth. A second pouch, slightly larger, contains field rations. The belt buckle bears a small Vanguard stamp — the same gauntlet emblem, pressed into the iron.

**Shoulders — Full Plate Pauldrons (Heavy):** Two steel pauldrons of four overlapping lames each — larger and more fully covering than standard Vanguard issue, extending from the neck to the outer upper arm and curving forward over the front of the shoulder. The left pauldron (shield arm) is noticeably larger, its lowest lame reaching nearly to the elbow, providing a wall of protection for the side most exposed during shield-forward fighting. The right pauldron is slightly smaller to preserve sword-arm mobility but still substantial. Each pauldron carries the same restrained linear scrollwork as the cuirass — shallow etched arcs following the curve of the lames, visible in raking light. The top lame of each pauldron has a raised border ridge along its upper edge — a structural reinforcement that also serves as a decorative accent, catching light as a clean bright line against the blue-gray steel. The edges of both pauldrons show combat wear — nicks and bright scratches where the steel has been freshly exposed by blade contact, the etching interrupted in places by gouges. The pauldrons are attached to the cuirass at the shoulder hinges with visible steel pins, and secured further by dark leather straps under the arm.

### Arms & Hands
**Forearms — Plate Vambraces over Mail:** Steel vambraces covering the forearms from wrist to elbow, strapped over the chainmail sleeves. The vambraces are two-piece clamshell construction — an upper and lower half hinged on the outer edge and buckled on the inner, allowing quick removal. The left vambrace integrates with the shield grip, providing a stable mounting point for the shield's arm strap.

**Hands — Plate Gauntlets (Heavy):** Full articulated plate gauntlets extending from mid-forearm to fingertip, matching the armor's ornamental quality. ### Worn Details & Storytelling Elements
The color palette is Vanguard steel and shadow: blue-gray plate, iron-gray mail, dark brown leather straps, and the subtle catch of light in the etched scrollwork that gives the armor a quality of restrained craftsmanship. This is not decorative parade armor — the ornamentation is shallow, linear, and subordinate to the armor's function — but it elevates the figure above rank-and-file soldiery. The overall impression is of a tall, armored warrior whose face is completely hidden behind the greathelm's visor — anonymous, imposing, the individuality of the person inside erased by the steel that covers them. The armor is maintained to a high standard — oiled, free of rust, buckles secured — but it is not pristine. Combat damage is present across every surface: dents, gouges, bright scratches that interrupt the etching. This is equipment that has been tested and has held.

The Relic Mark is invisible — on the chest, beneath the hauberk, the padding, and the plate. Nobody can see it. Everything hidden says person. The tension between those two readings is the visual story.

## VISUAL GENERATION NOTES (For All Three Characters)

**Lighting:** Overcast daylight — the Vale's default. Flat, ambient, gray-warm. This is the light that makes these characters look real rather than dramatic. No strong directional light, no god rays, no backlight halos. The light should be honest.

**Background:** A neutral environment that reads as "Severant Vale" without dominating — an out-of-focus treeline or stone wall, the suggestion of a muddy road or a gate behind them. The character is the subject. The world provides context without competing.

**Stance:** Each character should be in a neutral readiness pose — not mid-action, not at parade rest. The posture of someone who has stopped walking to assess something ahead of them. Weight balanced. Weapons accessible but not raised. Eyes forward.

**Skin & Texture Quality:** Photorealistic. Pores, weathering, imperfections, sweat, dirt. Skin looks like it belongs on a body that has been outdoors in autumn weather for weeks. Clean skin reads as wrong in this world.

**Material Quality:** Every surface should read as a real material with real weight. Leather looks like leather — you can see the grain, the staining, the wear pattern. Iron looks like iron — not silver, not chrome, but the specific dark matte of maintained steel. Cloth shows weave, wear, and the specific way fabric folds under its own weight.

**Scale Reference:** All three characters should be plausible as human beings standing between 1.7m and 1.85m. No heroic proportions. Armor sits on a real body with real proportions — it adds bulk where it covers and leaves visible transitions where different layers meet.

**What To Avoid:** No glowing effects (except the faint Relic Mark on the mage). No dramatic capes billowing in non-existent wind. No impossibly clean equipment. No oversized weapons that a person couldn't actually wield. No fantasy-game color saturation — the palette is muted, autumnal, grounded. These are people, not action figures.
