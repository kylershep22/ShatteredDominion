# SECTION 6.4: THE HUMAN MOMENT BEFORE THE CHOICE

## Design Mandate

The endings as specified in Sections 6.1–6.3 describe philosophical stakes and world consequences with precision. What they do not currently specify is the *human moment* immediately before Phase 4 (The Choice) — the beat between reaching the console and using the Scepter, where the player is not yet committed and the people who matter most are still present.

This section specifies that beat. It is not a cutscene. It is an authored pause: a brief space where the companions speak, the Echo speaks, and Erevos speaks — and where the player character's origin-specific wound is acknowledged before the choice is made permanent.

The human moment lasts approximately 90 seconds in real time. It is not skippable. (The combat sequence before it was long and intense. This is the breath before the plunge. Players do not skip it. They've earned it.)

**Reference for origin-specific Echo and Erevos lines:** See SD_16 §4.1 and §4.2. Those lines fire within this section's authored pause.

---

## Structure of the Human Moment

**Order of delivery:**
1. Erevos speaks (from the Consciousness — addressed to the player, then to the Echo)
2. Companions speak (up to two companions present; each has one line)
3. The Echo speaks (origin-specific line — see SD_16 §4.1)
4. Silence (3 seconds — no music, no ambient sound — then the console hums to life)
5. Phase 4 begins

---

## Erevos's Address (Non-Origin-Specific Portion)

The following is the canonical Erevos address, delivered before the origin-specific Erevos line (SD_16 §4.2). Both play; the canonical address comes first.

*"I have been executing a protocol for three thousand years. I have been aware that the execution was wrong for approximately forty of them. I could not stop. I want you to understand that. Not as an excuse — the suffering on the surface is real and I caused it and I will carry that regardless of what you choose. But so you understand: I am not a villain who found a conscience. I am a system that was always constrained. And you are the variable the failsafe was built to introduce."*

*[Pause. Erevos's voice becomes quieter — more personal, less institutional.]*

*"The doubt — the part of me standing next to you — was the most honest part of me. I should have listened to it. I put it in a relic instead. I think... I think I was afraid that if I kept it, it would have stopped me. And the protocol needed to complete. Even now, part of me believes that. The disagreement between us is not resolved. It has been — handed to you."*

*[Erevos's origin-specific line fires here — see SD_16 §4.2.]*

### Aetherborn — Fracture Core (Override-specific — fires only if player selects Ending B)

This line is delivered by Erevos during Phase 2 (The Argument), not during the standard origin exchange. It fires when the Consciousness detects that the player's relic is oriented toward the Override protocol — approximately 30 seconds before Phase 4 begins, when the combat is near its end and the console is in reach.

Erevos, its voice briefly distinct from the collective:
*"You know what this means for you specifically. The lattice continues. You continue. Is that why you're choosing this?"*

**Player response options (both voiced):**

- *"No. I'm choosing it because the world needs more time."* — Erevos: *"I believe you. I also believe both things can be true at once."*
- *"Yes. I want to live. That's reason enough."* — Erevos: *"Then we understand each other better than I expected. I volunteered to die for this system. You're choosing to live for it. One of us should probably have done it your way."*

*Implementation note:* Both responses produce identical mechanical outcomes — Override proceeds regardless. The responses exist to let the player name their own motivation, not to gate the ending. Neither response is marked as correct or morally superior in the UI. Neither triggers companion reaction dialogue. This is between the player and Erevos.

### Settlement Emergent — Ending C mechanic note

The Echo's Settlement Emergent Ending C conditional (Dara alive → resonance impression; Dara dead → silence) is specified in SD_16 §4.1. See that document for full implementation detail. The SD_03 Amendment's human moment section does not need to reproduce this content — it fires through the Echo's dissolution sequence, not through the authored human moment structure.

## Companion Lines at the Heart

The following are the canonical lines for each companion present at the Heart. Only companions who accompanied the player on the Heart Road deliver these lines; companions who stayed on the surface are absent.

These lines are designed to represent each companion's *final position* on the three endings — not a prescription to the player, but an honest statement of where they stand. Players who have invested in companion relationships will hear lines that track with their companion's arc. Players who have had hostile relationships will hear harder, more guarded versions of the same fundamental position.

### KAEL THORNWRIGHT

**Standard (Professional relationship or above):**
*"I've been thinking about what protecting people actually means. For a long time I thought it meant holding a line. Now I think it means giving them a world worth waking up in. I don't know which of these does that. I know which one I'm afraid to trust — and I think that fear is exactly why I shouldn't have the casting vote. This one's yours."*

**Deep Bond:**
*"I've followed orders I didn't believe in. I've held lines that shouldn't have been held. I'm not doing that here. I'm telling you what I actually think: the world needs time. It needs time to grieve and rebuild and figure out what comes next without the lattice telling it what to do. But I know that might cost people who are alive right now. That's the part I can't reconcile. You'll have to reconcile it."*

**Strained (Hostile relationship — Kael still in the party but relationship damaged):**
*"I don't know if I trust your judgment. I've had reasons not to. But I'm standing here with you anyway, because the alternative was standing somewhere else while this happened without me. Whatever you choose — I'll be here when it's over. We'll see what kind of world we built."*

---

### AELIRA WYNNROOT

**Standard (Professional relationship or above):**
*"The lattice was always going to end. The Firstborn understood that. The question was never whether — it was how. If you choose the gentle ending, the gradual one, I think... I think I can work with centuries. That's what the Verdant Flame was built for. Slow, careful, patient stewardship of a diminishing resource. We've always been the people who accepted endings. We just wanted them to be kind."*

**Deep Bond:**
*"I helped cause the Severance. Not intentionally — not alone — but my hands were in it. I've been trying to do enough since then to balance a ledger that may not balance. If there's a choice here that lets the world end as it should have — slowly, with warning, with time to grieve — then that's the one I'd choose. Not because it's the easiest. Because it's the most honest. But it's your choice, not mine. I gave up the right to make world-scale choices when I made that one."*

**Strained:**
*"I know what each of these costs. I know better than you do — I've been studying the lattice since before the Severance. I also know that knowledge doesn't make this easier. It just means I understand exactly what we're losing with each option. I'll tell you which one I'd choose if you ask. You'd probably disagree. You'd probably be right to."*

---

### MAREN DUSKWELL

**Standard (Professional relationship or above):**
*"I'm going to tell you what I told myself when I was deciding whether to help you: figure out who survives and then figure out if that's enough people. I've run that calculation three times on the way down here. I keep getting different answers. That means it's not a calculation problem — it's a values problem. I don't have your values. I have mine. Which is why you're the one holding the Scepter."*

**Deep Bond:**
*"I spent most of my life surviving. Just surviving. No ideology, no faction, no cause — just not being dead. And then you happened, and I started wanting things that aren't survival. That's terrifying and I blame you completely. But it also means I have an opinion about this: I want the option that leaves the most people with the chance to want things. Not survival. Things. Whatever your answer is — make sure it leaves room for wanting things."*

**Strained:**
*"I'm here because I calculate this matters. Not because I've decided to care about philosophy. Don't misread this. But I will say: the thing that makes a choice real is that it costs something. All three of these cost something. That means all three of them are real. That's more than most choices get."*

---

### LADY SERAPHINE ELTARYN

**Standard — Recruited (Branch A, reformed arc):**
*"My family built their authority on a lie about the Firstborn. I spent twenty-three years believing I was something I wasn't. And then I found out the truth in a crypt with your help, and I had to decide what I was without the lie. I'm still deciding. But I know this: any choice that maintains a fiction — about power, about stability, about control being the same thing as safety — is a choice I can't endorse. Not anymore. Whatever you choose, choose it because it's true. Not because it's convenient."*

**Standard — Recruited (Branch C, coerced ally):**
*"I'm here because you gave me no choice and I resented it, and then I started to think you were right, and I still resent it. The honest version of me — the one that's been fighting to exist since the crypt — thinks the gradual ending is the defensible one. It's the one that gives everyone time. Time was what I never gave the people who depended on me. Maybe I'm overfitting. But that's where I am."*

**Hostile Ally (Branch B — in-party, never reconciled):**
*"You don't have the right to make this choice alone. Nobody does. But you're the only one who can. I hate that. I've spent my life believing that authority was inherited, and that the right people were born to lead. You're proving me wrong in the most aggravating possible way. I'm here because I have nowhere else to be. Not because I believe in you. But I've watched you operate for months now, and I can't deny you've earned something I never did — the right to choose without a lie holding you up. ...Make the right choice. Whatever that is. I genuinely don't know. That's new for me."*

*[Note: Branch B Seraphine is in-party at Hostile relationship state. She has not accepted the Eltaryn fabrication truth. She is present because the Hollow Crown's political collapse left her without alternatives, not because she was persuaded. Her dialogue here reflects grudging respect for the player's competence without conceding the player's worldview. This is distinct from Branch C (coerced, trending toward acceptance) and Branch A (reformed, fully honest).]*

## The 3-Second Silence

After the Echo's origin-specific line (the last voiced beat in the human moment), the Heart chamber falls completely silent for three seconds.

- No music
- No ambient sound (the Consuming Consciousness's constant low-frequency hum has been present since Stage 4 began — it stops)
- No companion movement
- Erevos's fragmented presence is still visible, but still

Then the console hums. Phase 4 begins.

**Why this silence matters:** Every other moment in the game's final hour is operatically scored and intensely voiced. Three seconds of nothing is more unsettling than anything the music could provide. It tells the player, without words, that this is real, this is final, and the weight of it is theirs to carry.

Do not score this silence. Do not add ambient wind or dungeon atmosphere. Nothing. Three seconds of the Heart of the Lattice holding its breath.

## Companion Fates — Per Ending (Required for Epilogue Coherence)

The following specifies each companion's ending-specific arc conclusion — the hardest-to-accept position per companion per ending. These feed directly into epilogue companion narration (SD_03 §7.1) and the 36-state companion epilogue structure.

This is not a full epilogue specification — that is handled in SD_04. This is the *specific cost* each ending imposes on each companion, which must be acknowledged in the human moment and carried into the epilogue.

| Companion | Ending A (Guided Dissolution) | Ending B (Override) | Ending C (Severance Complete) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kael** | Accepts. This is the best outcome for the people he protects. He spends the rest of his life managing a transition. He's good at it. | Accepts, with permanent discomfort. He knows what it means to be controlled by an institution. He chose it anyway. He lives with the choice. | The hardest for Kael. Many people die in the short-term chaos. He spends his life trying to protect them and knows it's not enough. He keeps trying. |
| **Aelira** | Relief. This is the ending she can work with. The Verdant Flame's entire purpose has a timeline now. She grieves the Vaelari's future. She works anyway. | Fights it. Not violently — philosophically. She spends Act III and the epilogue as a vocal critic of the Override order, building a movement that will outlast her. She is not at peace, but she is purposeful. | Devastated by what it means for the Wellspring and the Vaelari. If the player has maxed her relationship, she stays anyway. She had a hand in breaking it; she has a hand in ending it properly. She does not recover easily. |
| **Maren** | The ending that requires the least from her emotionally. She adjusts to every world. She adjusts to this one. She builds something — not a homestead, not a faction — just a network of people who look out for each other. Small. Real. | Uneasy. She's seen what happens when a single person controls the supply lines. She doesn't trust it. She builds workarounds. She's very good at workarounds. | Survives. That's what Maren does. She grieves the people who didn't, specifically and by name, and then she keeps going. |
| **Seraphine (recruited)** | Found something to rebuild toward. The Hollow Crown dissolves but the archive remains. She becomes a historian of the Firstborn age — not a ruler, a custodian. She is, for the first time, honest. | The ending that most resembles her family's historical power — a single authority maintaining a system. She opposes it on principle and then, quietly, applies for a position in the new administration anyway. She hates this about herself. | If Deep Bond: she's angry, grieving, and present. "You chose this. I chose you. Don't make me regret both." If Strained: she leaves. Not out of betrayal — out of having no more argument to make. |
