# THE SHATTERED DOMINION — PROFESSIONAL ORDERS SYSTEM

## Document 14 | Version 1.0 | Depends on: World Bible & Lore Foundation, Faction Playbook, Combat & Build Framework, Loot & Itemization Bible, Economy System, Companion Design

**Purpose:** This document defines the professional order system — three joinable institutions that provide the player with a non-political professional identity, a curated questline, exclusive mechanical rewards, and social recognition throughout the Vale. Every other document that references order membership, order-exclusive abilities, order NPC behavior, or professional identity defers to this one.

**Design Reference:** Oblivion's structured rank hierarchies with skill requirements (restored by community consensus as the correct model). Skyrim's narrative-driven questlines (without Skyrim's failure to gate advancement behind relevant skills). The Witcher's treatment of professional identity as social currency. Post-Severance institutional survival as a grounding principle — these orders exist because people organized around shared expertise when the world broke.

---

# SECTION 1: DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## 1.1 The Core Distinction — Factions Are Politics, Orders Are Profession

The faction system answers: *What should the world become?*
The professional order system answers: *What am I, and who recognizes it?*

A player can be Vanguard-aligned politically and a member of the Athenaeum professionally. Their faction is their ideology. Their order is their craft. These two systems must never collapse into each other.

**What this means mechanically:**

- Order membership has **no mechanical interaction** with faction standing. Joining or advancing in an order does not cost Fame, gain Infamy, or shift ideology scores with any faction.
- Orders are **apolitical institutions** that predate the Severance and persist despite it. Every order has members from every faction. Internal order politics exist, but they are professional disputes, not faction proxy wars.
- A player can be a member of **one order at a time.** This is a meaningful identity commitment — not a restriction on gameplay, but a lens through which the player experiences a curated questline, earns exclusive abilities, and gains social recognition from the world.
- **Ambient narrative friction** exists between order membership and faction alignment. Order NPCs are aware of the player's political affiliations and produce reactive dialogue. This friction is purely narrative — dialogue, overheard conversations, shifts in tone. It never produces mechanical penalties. It produces discomfort, and that discomfort is the point.

## 1.2 What Players Want From This System

1. **A rank ladder that feels earned and unpredictable.** Named titles. Clear progression. The world treating you differently at each tier. But also structural breaks — ranks where the order asks something unexpected, tests a different dimension of competence, or challenges the player's assumptions about what the order values. A ladder that is purely "do more quests + have higher stats" teaches the player the formula by Rank 3 and loses its power.

2. **Skill-gated advancement.** Advancement in SD orders requires demonstrated competence in the relevant skills — not just quest completion. If the Athenaeum is an arcane institution, your Intelligence, your spell use-rank, and your Runecarving tier matter.

3. **A physical home.** A headquarters that evolves as you advance. A place with NPCs who know you, workstations you can use, and a bed that's yours.

4. **A self-contained narrative arc with early moral texture.** 15–25 hours of curated content per order that tells a complete story with its own central conflict, its own moral question, and its own climax. The central moral question arrives in Act II, but the questline signals that it will ask hard questions by Quest 3.

5. **Exclusive mechanical rewards that change how you play.** Abilities, crafting recipes, gear, and passive bonuses that are **only** available through order advancement and cannot be replicated through faction standing, loot drops, or other systems. The mid-tier rewards (Rank 3) must include a gameplay-transformative ability — something that changes the player's combat or exploration approach.

6. **Visible professional identity.** A cosmetic marker — pin, sigil, toolmark, trophy cord — that is visible on the player character and recognized by NPCs. Designed alongside the rank structure.

## 1.3 What This System Must NOT Do

- **Compete with factions for quest volume.** Factions already have 36+ quests across three acts. Orders have 12 quests each — enough for identity, not enough to dilute.
- **Create a second reputation grind.** Order rank is quest-gated and skill-gated, not a numerical meter. There is no Order Fame.
- **Gate the main story.** No main quest requires order membership. Orders are a parallel enrichment track.
- **Make non-members feel punished.** A player who joins no order still has a complete, satisfying game. Orders add depth — they don't withhold basics.
- **Be a vending machine.** Orders have standards, expectations, and consequences for violating them. The player's relationship with their order has tension after joining, not just before.

# SECTION 2: THE THREE PROFESSIONAL ORDERS

## 2.1 Why Three

Three orders map cleanly onto the three major playstyle archetypes the Combat Framework already supports: arcane mastery, martial/survival expertise, and engineering/applied knowledge specialization. Three is enough for differentiated playthroughs without creating a development burden that competes with faction content for resources. Each order is substantial. None are filler.

---

## 2.2 The Athenaeum of the Fracture

*"To understand the wound is to understand the world that made it."*

### Identity

Arcane scholars, lattice researchers, and practical magicians. The Athenaeum existed before the Severance as a loose network of independent researchers and hedge mages who operated outside the Dominion's formal magical institutions (which were controlled by the aristocracy). Post-Severance, they have consolidated — the crisis gave them urgency and purpose they never had before. They are the only institution in the Vale that treats magic as a discipline to be studied rather than a political tool to be controlled (Vanguard), a natural force to be revered (Verdant Flame), or a commodity to be exploited (Reavers).

### Headquarters

The Fractured Hall — a repurposed pre-Severance lecture hall in Hollowford's Market Quarter, expanded into the basement levels of an adjacent collapsed building. The upper floor is a library and workshop. The lower levels contain a lattice research chamber built around a stable micro-fracture that the Athenaeum deliberately maintains for study. This micro-fracture is the Athenaeum's most valuable asset and their most dangerous secret.

### Leadership

**Archivist Thessaly Crane** (human, 50s, former Dominion academy instructor) — Practical, dry-humored, exhausted. She built the Athenaeum from nothing in the six months after the Severance. Not a visionary — an organizer who recognized that if someone didn't centralize magical knowledge preservation, it would be lost within a generation. She is the questline's primary mentor figure and, eventually, the person whose judgment the player must evaluate when her pragmatism collides with ethics.

**Lyris Half-Sung** (Vaelari, Driftless, early 30s equivalent) — The Athenaeum's most talented natural caster and Thessaly's chosen successor. Lyris's research into Wellspring-lattice interaction is producing results that are genuinely dangerous. She is not reckless — she is brilliant and impatient, which is worse.

**Tomas Inkwell** (human, 20s, former Hollow Crown archive clerk) — The Athenaeum's librarian and records keeper. Defected from the Hollow Crown because he believed knowledge should be shared, not hoarded. Seraphine considers him a traitor. He is terrified she is right.

### What They Offer the Player

- Advanced understanding of lattice mechanics (lore codex unlocks)
- Exclusive spell modifications and arcane passive abilities
- Runecarving advancement bonuses
- Access to the micro-fracture research chamber (unique crafting station for lattice-infused items)
- Social recognition: NPCs throughout the Vale recognize Athenaeum members and respond accordingly — scholars with respect, Vanguard hardliners with suspicion, Hollow Crown agents with contempt

### Visual Identity Marker

The Athenaeum Pin — a small silver-and-blue geometric design representing the lattice fracture pattern. Worn on the left breast. Rank 1–2: plain silver. Rank 3–4: silver with a faint blue luminescence in the fracture lines. Rank 5: the fracture lines pulse with visible lattice energy, subtly but unmistakably. Visible in conversation close-ups and at medium distance.

**Thesis Defense (Rank 3 Structural Break):**
After completing Quest 6, the player is summoned to present a theory about a lattice phenomenon they have been studying throughout the questline. Thessaly, Lyris, and Tomas are present. The encounter is dialogue-driven — the player selects from interpretations of data and observations gathered during Quests 1–6.

The dialogue options reference **things the player experienced during quests**, not codex text. The player observed the micro-fracture's behavior in Quest 3. They saw Lyris's experiment results in Quest 4. They documented lattice energy signatures in Quest 5. The defense asks the player to interpret those experiences — "What did the energy signature pattern at the fracture boundary suggest to you?" — with multiple interpretations that map to different analytical frameworks. The lore codex entries provide deeper context that makes the defense *richer* for players who engaged with them, but the baseline path is built entirely from quest experience. No player who paid attention during gameplay hits a wall here.

Wrong answers don't permanently fail the player, but produce a "revise and resubmit" state — Thessaly assigns one additional field observation quest before the defense can be reattempted.

### Questline Arc

**Quest 1 — "The Burning Index" (Act I, Early)**
Entry quest. A pre-Severance library in the Market Quarter is being dismantled for building materials. Thessaly asks the player to retrieve specific texts before they're destroyed. The quest introduces the Fractured Hall, the NPC leadership, and the Athenaeum's mission. Straightforward — this establishes the order's identity.

**Quest 2 — "Field Notes" (Act I)**
Protect an Athenaeum field researcher conducting surveys near corruption zone boundaries. The researcher is cataloging lattice energy signatures. Combat encounter with corrupted wildlife. The player sees Athenaeum methods in practice — observation before action, data collection even during danger.

**Quest 3 — "The Dying Frequency" (Act I — First Moral Choice)**
The micro-fracture is discovered during a routine survey of the Fractured Hall's lower levels. A dying researcher — Aldric Sorren, who has been conducting unauthorized experiments in the basement for weeks — has partial data on the fracture's behavior. His experiments were unsanctioned and potentially dangerous, but his data is valuable. Sorren will only share his complete notes if the player agrees to suppress the unauthorized nature of his research from Thessaly.

**The choice:** Honor Sorren's request (suppress the unauthorized experiments, present the data as a routine discovery) or report honestly to Thessaly (Sorren's reputation is destroyed, but the Athenaeum's institutional integrity is preserved). Neither option is punished mechanically. Both have consequences: suppressing the truth earns Tomas's quiet approval and Lyris's respect, but if Thessaly discovers the deception later, her trust is shaken. Reporting honestly earns Thessaly's deep trust and Sorren's bitter resentment — his contributions are erased from the record.

**Quest 4 — "Unstable Harmonics" (Act I, Late)**
Lyris's Wellspring-lattice experiments produce results the Verdant Flame would consider heretical and the Vanguard would consider illegal. The player assists with an experiment that succeeds — and in succeeding, reveals that the boundary between lattice study and lattice manipulation is thinner than anyone realized. The Athenaeum now holds knowledge every faction would want to control. Thessaly imposes secrecy protocols. Lyris is exhilarated. Tomas begins locking the archives.

**Quest 5 — "The Cartographer's Fear" (Act II, Early)**
An Athenaeum mapping team has gone silent in the Verdantheart border corruption zone. The player investigates and discovers a lattice node actively rewriting the corruption pattern around it — not spreading corruption, but restructuring it into something organized. The team's cartographer fled because the pattern looks intentional. Something is directing the corruption's growth. The data recovered here feeds directly into the main story's Act II revelations.

**Quest 6 — "Competing Offers" (Act II)**
Three pressures arrive simultaneously: the Vanguard demands oversight of all lattice research. The Hollow Crown offers "partnership" (absorption in all but name). The Verdant Flame sends Aelira (if she is a companion, this creates a unique intersection) to investigate the micro-fracture's safety. The player must help Thessaly navigate — choosing which threat to address first, which to stall, and which to concede ground on. No option eliminates all three threats.

**Thesis Defense occurs here — between Quest 6 and Quest 7.**

**Quest 7 — "The Lyris Question" (Act II — Moral Pivot)**
Lyris produces a breakthrough: a technique that could stabilize corruption zones by rewriting lattice energy patterns from within. The technique works. It is also disturbingly similar to the methodology the Firstborn used when they created the Consuming Consciousness. Thessaly wants to halt the research. Lyris argues the technique is fundamentally different. The player must choose: support Thessaly's caution (research freezes, corruption zones continue expanding, but the risk of repeating the Firstborn's mistake is eliminated) or support Lyris's urgency (research continues under the player's oversight, but the ethical line has been crossed).

**Quest 8 — "The Archive War" (Act II, Late)**
The Hollow Crown escalates. Seraphine (or her agents) attempts to acquire Athenaeum research through back channels — purchasing loyalty from a junior researcher, accessing the Fractured Hall through Tomas's former Hollow Crown credentials. The player investigates the breach and discovers that Tomas's "defection" may have been orchestrated by Seraphine as a planted mole. Tomas may not have known this. Or he may have always known and convinced himself it didn't matter. The confrontation with Tomas is one of the questline's most emotionally complex scenes.

**Quest 9 — "Applied Theory" (Act III, Early)**
The player's aligned faction requests specific lattice data to support their Act III objectives. Thessaly asks the player to evaluate whether the request serves the Vale or just serves the faction. The player can provide the data (strengthening faction position, compromising Athenaeum neutrality), provide modified data (a half-measure), or refuse (maintaining order integrity, straining faction relationship narratively — no mechanical penalty).

**Quest 10 — "Fracture Bloom" (Act III)**
The micro-fracture is destabilizing. Either Lyris's experiments (if supported) or external interference (if research was halted) has pushed the fracture beyond stable parameters. The player must restabilize the fracture through a complex lattice manipulation sequence or seal the chamber permanently.

**Quest 11 — "What We Built" (Act III)**
The Athenaeum's future depends on cumulative choices. A meeting of all members where the order's future is debated. The player's rank, relationships, and demonstrated values determine which options are available and how the vote resolves.

**Quest 12 — "The Archon's Question" (Act III — Resolution)**
The Athenaeum's traditional final examination: Thessaly asks: "What is the most dangerous thing we have learned, and what should we do with it?" The player's answer determines the Athenaeum's institutional stance in the epilogue — whether it becomes a public institution, a guarded secret society, or something the player defines.

**Central Moral Question:** *Is understanding dangerous knowledge better than suppressing it? Where is the line between research and recklessness?*

## 2.3 The Wardens of the Reach

*"The land has teeth. We are the ones who count them."*

### Identity

Professional monster hunters, corruption zone navigators, and wilderness specialists. Not soldiers (that's the Vanguard), not nature-worshippers (that's the Verdant Flame), not survivalists (that's just living in the Vale). The Wardens are experts in a specific and dangerous profession: understanding, tracking, and killing the creatures that the Severance unleashed. They are the closest thing the Vale has to Witchers — professional, knowledgeable, and viewed by the general population with a mixture of gratitude, fear, and social discomfort.

### Headquarters

The Reach House — a fortified lodge on the northern road between Hollowford and Ironwood, built into the ruins of a pre-Severance waystation. Deliberately located outside any settlement's walls. The Wardens do not want to be part of a town. They want to be between towns, where the work is.

### Leadership

**Warden-Captain Brenna Ashmark** (human, 40s, former Vanguard ranger who resigned her commission) — Brenna left the Vanguard because they treated corruption zones as a military problem. They aren't. Corruption-spawned creatures don't respond to suppression tactics, patrol routes, or force projection. They respond to knowledge. Brenna built the Wardens around that principle. She is competent, unsentimental, and privately carrying a wound — she lost two Wardens in the Verdantheart border zone and has not led a hunt personally since.

**Fen** (Aetherborn, Settlement Emergent, apparent age 20s) — The Wardens' corruption zone specialist. Fen's Aetherborn physiology gives them an intuitive sense of lattice disturbance that no amount of training can replicate. They are invaluable and they know it. Their relationship with the order is complicated — they are respected for what they can do and subtly othered for what they are.

**Old Marsh** (human, 60s, retired hunter, now the lodge's bestiary keeper) — Marsh has fought more creature types than anyone alive in the Vale. He can no longer hunt, but his knowledge of creature behavior, weaknesses, and habitat patterns is the Wardens' institutional memory. He speaks slowly, remembers everything, and is the only person Brenna defers to without question.

### What They Offer the Player

- Bestiary system unlocks (detailed creature entries with mechanical weaknesses, behavioral patterns, and habitat data)
- Exclusive combat abilities focused on preparation and exploitation (traps, lures, creature-specific weapon coatings, environmental awareness)
- Corruption zone navigation tools (safe path identification, corruption resistance buffs)
- Survival and tracking skill bonuses
- Social recognition: Wardens are respected in frontier settlements and viewed as useful eccentrics in Hollowford

### Visual Identity Marker

The Warden's Cord — a braided leather cord worn across the chest, shoulder to hip. Trophy tokens are added at each rank: a claw at Rank 1, a fang at Rank 2, a scale at Rank 3, a bone shard at Rank 4, and a corrupted crystal fragment at Rank 5. Frontier settlement NPCs comment on the cord's weight as the player advances.

### Rank Structure

**The Silent Hunt (Rank 3 Structural Break):**
After completing Quest 6, the player is assigned a Stalker's Trial: track a corruption-zone apex creature — a Lattice Prowler — for a full quest without engaging it. Map its patrol route. Identify feeding behavior. Document its lair. Record its reaction to environmental stimuli. If the player attacks the creature at any point, they fail and must wait for a new Prowler to establish territory (narrative cooldown — approximately 3 in-game days). This tests the Warden philosophy that understanding precedes killing and forces combat-oriented players to experience the discipline the order actually values. The data gathered directly informs the Great Hunt preparation in Act III.

The Silent Hunt at Rank 3 is deliberately placed before the Hunter's Mark ability unlock — the player proves they can observe patiently before they receive the tool that mechanically rewards observation.

### Questline Arc

**Quest 1 — "The Blood Trail" (Act I, Early)**
Entry quest. A creature has attacked travelers on the northern road. Brenna assigns the player to track it. Straightforward tracking exercise — follow signs, identify the creature type, locate its den, eliminate it. Introduces the Reach House, NPC leadership, and Warden methodology.

**Quest 2 — "Preparation Is Survival" (Act I)**
A more dangerous creature in the eastern forest. Brenna forbids engaging until the player completes preparation: identify the creature type through tracks and scat, consult Old Marsh for weakness data, craft coatings and set traps. The player who charges in without preparing faces a genuinely harder fight. The player who prepares finds the encounter manageable. Teaches the doctrine: knowledge before violence.

**Quest 3 — "The Mother's Teeth" (Act I — First Moral Choice)**
The player's first corruption-zone hunt. A corrupted wolf matriarch threatening Grainfell livestock. The moral complexity arrives at the end: the matriarch was protecting a den of uncorrupted pups inside the corruption zone boundary — a boundary that is expanding.

**The choice:** Kill the pups (they will inevitably corrupt — Brenna's position, stated coldly and practically) or attempt relocation (move them outside the boundary, requiring additional time, resources, and risk with no guarantee of success — Fen's position, stated with quiet intensity). The player's choice shapes how Brenna and Fen engage with them for the rest of the questline.

**Quest 4 — "What the Corruption Makes" (Act I, Late)**
Old Marsh identifies a creature from a trophy that doesn't match any known bestiary entry. Something new is evolving in the corruption zones — creatures that aren't corrupted versions of existing animals but entirely novel organisms showing signs of organized behavior. Introduces the Great Hunt concept.

**Quest 5 — "The Rancher's Grief" (Act II, Early)**
A frontier rancher's livestock is killed by something that hunts at night and leaves no tracks. Investigation reveals the predator is a corrupted human — still partially sentient, trying to feed a family that fled months ago. The player must handle a target that is both dangerous predator and suffering person. Brenna's doctrine has no answer for this.

**Quest 6 — "Migration" (Act II)**
Corruption zone creatures are moving in coordinated migration patterns suggesting a directing intelligence. The player's team encounters a concentration of novel creatures behaving like a military unit: scouts, flankers, and a command creature. The fight is the most tactically demanding combat encounter in the order questline to this point.

**The Silent Hunt occurs here — between Quest 6 and Quest 7.**

**Quest 7 — "The Veteran's Return" (Act II — Moral Pivot)**
Brenna asks the player to investigate the Verdantheart border zone where she lost two Wardens. The player discovers a Firstborn bio-construct, reactivated by the Severance, operating as the corruption zone's apex predator. Originally a benign ecosystem regulator, now corrupted, enforcing a lethal version of "balance." The moral pivot: destroying it would destabilize the zone and potentially trigger uncontrolled expansion. Leaving it alive means accepting that a machine decides what lives and dies. The player's assessment determines whether the Great Hunt targets the construct for destruction or capture.

**Quest 8 — "Brenna's Walk" (Act II, Late)**
Brenna enters the field again. The player accompanies her to the site where she lost her Wardens. Not a combat quest — a character quest. Brenna needs to see what killed her people, understand it, and decide whether she is still fit to lead. What Brenna decides shapes the order's Act III arc.

**Quest 9 — "The Staging Ground" (Act III, Early)**
Preparation for the Great Hunt. Establish forward camps, lay trap networks, identify the construct's patrol patterns, recruit available allies. A full quest dedicated to planning a single encounter. Every preparation step provides a tangible advantage in the Great Hunt.

**Quest 10 — "The Great Hunt" (Act III — Climactic)**
Multi-stage boss encounter — the most mechanically demanding content in the order system. Uses terrain, traps, and intelligence gathered throughout the questline. Destruction path: full kill encounter with phases testing different Warden skills. Capture path: harder, non-lethal, requiring lattice manipulation techniques.

**Quest 11 — "What It Was" (Act III)**
What the construct's remains (or captured body) reveal about the lattice's current state. The player's faction requests access to construct data. Brenna asks whether the Wardens should share it.

**Quest 12 — "The Next Hunt" (Act III — Resolution)**
The succession question. Brenna must decide whether to continue leading or formalize the player's authority. The player's relationships and cumulative choices determine available outcomes.

**Central Moral Question:** *What does it mean to be a professional killer in a world where the things you kill may not be what they seem? Is the construct an enemy or a symptom?*

## 2.4 The Anvil Compact

*"The world broke. We mend."*

### Identity

Engineers, structural specialists, and applied problem-solvers. The Anvil Compact is not a crafting guild in the sense of "people who make things at a workbench." It is a professional order of people who go where things are broken and fix them — often in dangerous conditions, often under time pressure, and increasingly with materials that fight back. Pre-Severance, the Compact was a trade union regulating quality and pricing across the Dominion's craft industries. Post-Severance, it keeps Hollowford's walls from collapsing, frontier settlements' infrastructure operational, and the Vale's supply chains functional. Its members are combat engineers, field fabricators, and the only people in the region qualified to work with lattice-affected materials.

The Compact member's power fantasy is: **"The world is broken. I fix things. Often at personal risk, in places no one else will go, with tools I built myself."** A Compact field engineer crawling through a collapsing Firstborn conduit to repair a water intake while lattice energy bleeds through the walls is as dramatic as a Warden tracking a corruption-zone apex predator — the danger is different, not lesser.

### Headquarters

The Crucible — the largest building in Hollowford's Forge District. A combined workshop, testing facility, and guild hall containing the only Master Forge in the Vale: a pre-Severance installation producing materials no field forge can match. The Crucible maintains a materials library — samples of every alloy, wood type, leather treatment, and textile the Compact has documented, preserved for reference and research.

### Leadership

**Guildmaster Harmon Slade** (human, 50s, third-generation blacksmith) — Harmon believes a well-made tool is a moral act. Meticulous, stubborn, and deeply suspicious of anyone who treats crafting as a means to an end rather than an end in itself. His political neutrality is genuine — he has refused contracts from every faction because they wanted quantity over quality.

**Kira Embersong** (Ironkin-descended human, 30s, metallurgist and field engineer) — The Compact's most innovative member. Experimenting with lattice-infused alloys that could revolutionize construction or produce catastrophically unstable materials. She is the one the Compact sends into dangerous environments because she understands both the engineering and the arcane properties of what she's working with.

**Darvish** (Drakeling refugee, 40s, master leatherworker and structural specialist from the Crimson Flats) — Drakeling construction techniques are designed for volcanic instability, which translates directly to post-Severance structural challenges. His Drakeling heritage makes some clients refuse his work. His skill makes him irreplaceable. He speaks rarely, produces work that silences every argument, and is quietly building a network of Drakeling apprentices.

### What They Offer the Player

- Crafting skill advancement bonuses (XP multiplier for crafting activities)
- Exclusive crafting recipes at each rank
- Access to the Master Forge
- Compact Certification system (certified items sell for 20% more to all merchants)
- Materials library access (hidden material properties and optimal combination data)
- Field engineering abilities that are combat-relevant and exploration-relevant
- Social recognition: trusted by merchants, respected by builders, valued by every faction's quartermaster

### Visual Identity Marker

The Maker's Band — a metal wrist cuff on the dominant hand bearing the Compact's anvil sigil. Rank 1–2: plain iron. Rank 3–4: steel with an inlaid maker's mark (unique to the player — they choose the design during the Journeyman Trial). Rank 5: forged from the material used in their Magnum Opus. Visible during all crafting animations and in third-person gameplay.

**Journeyman Trial (Rank 2 Structural Break):**
The player is given specific materials and asked to produce a specific item — but the materials are deliberately imperfect (one component is corrupted, another is the wrong alloy). The test is not whether the player can follow a recipe but whether they can identify the problems, adapt their approach, and produce a functional result despite constraints. Harmon observes. His evaluation is qualitative — he comments on the player's decision-making, not just the output.

**The Blind Commission (Rank 4 Structural Break):**
The player is given a broken artifact of unknown origin. No recipe. Partially degraded materials. Function not immediately apparent. The player must analyze the construction (materials library knowledge), identify missing components (field sourcing from a dangerous location), determine original function (lore knowledge or independent research), and restore it. A multi-step problem-solving quest. The restored artifact becomes a permanent item with unique properties determined by how the player interpreted its function.

### Questline Arc

**Quest 1 — "The Assessment" (Act I, Early)**
Entry quest. Hollowford's south wall has stress fractures. The player assists Kira in documenting the damage, taking material samples, and identifying priority repair points. Introduces the Compact's methodology and establishes that "crafting" in the Compact context means maintaining the infrastructure that keeps people alive.

**Quest 2 — "Under Pressure" (Act I)**
The Forge District's primary water intake — running through a partially collapsed Firstborn conduit — is failing. The player fabricates replacement components at the Crucible, then installs them on-site while managing lattice exposure. The conduit is cramped, unstable, and the lattice energy interferes with metal properties in real time. First field engineering mission.

**Journeyman Trial occurs here — between Quest 2 and Quest 3.**

**Quest 3 — "Condemned" (Act I — First Moral Choice)**
An Ironwood timber supplier has been unknowingly selling corruption-tainted lumber. A frontier family's home is structurally compromised.

**The choice:** Report through Compact channels (family loses home, supplier is ruined — he didn't know, but Compact standards hold the seller responsible). Quietly replace the tainted timber yourself (violates reporting protocol, covers the supplier's failure, protects the family, but the supplier continues operating). Or a third path — report the corruption source to the Verdant Flame while privately arranging replacement lumber, addressing the root cause without destroying either party (requires the most time and effort, and the Compact is divided on this approach).

**Quest 4 — "The Hot Forge" (Act I, Late)**
A Vanguard border outpost's defensive wall is compromised by corruption-weakened stone. Field assessment, on-site fabrication, and repair completion before the next corruption surge. Combat engineering: the player works with tools while the corruption zone bleeds environmental hazards into the work site.

**Quest 5 — "The New Alloy" (Act II, Early)**
Kira's lattice-infused alloy experiments produce a material that is demonstrably superior to anything in the Compact's history. It requires Firstborn lattice fragments as a component. The player helps Kira source fragments and conduct the first full-scale test. Every faction will want this material.

**Quest 6 — "Supply Chain" (Act II)**
Three fragment sources: Verdant Flame purified fragments (safe, limited, politically controlled), Reaver black-market fragments (abundant, origins unknown, possibly tainted), or direct corruption zone harvesting (maximum quality, maximum danger). The player's choice determines the Compact's material supply chain and creates unavoidable faction entanglements.

**Quest 7 — "The Arms Question" (Act II — Moral Pivot)**
Compact-certified weapons made with the new alloy are reaching the market — all four factions purchasing for military purposes. Harmon is furious. The system worked exactly as designed; the problem is the system wasn't designed for a world at war. Harmon wants to restrict alloy weapon sales. Kira argues restriction is naive. Darvish points out that restrictions would disproportionately affect Drakeling communities. The player must advise. There is no clean answer.

**Quest 8 — "The Counterfeit" (Act II, Late)**
Compact-marked items are appearing in the market that the Compact did not produce. Counterfeit certification marks. The source: the player's aligned faction is reproducing the Compact's quality stamp on inferior goods. The player must confront the fact that their political allies are exploiting their professional institution.

**The Blind Commission occurs here — between Quest 8 and Quest 9.**

**Quest 9 — "Field Conditions" (Act III, Early)**
The most dangerous field engineering job in the Vale: a critical lattice conduit beneath the Broken Road (Corruption Zone 3) has degraded to imminent failure. Repair must be conducted inside an active corruption zone, at depth, with lattice energy actively interfering with materials and tools. The Compact's equivalent of the Great Hunt — everything the player has learned is tested simultaneously.

**Quest 10 — "The Magnum Opus" (Act III — Climactic)**
The player creates a single item representing the highest expression of their craft. The item is not specified — the player chooses what to make. The order judges ambition, execution, and intentionality. Harmon evaluates craftsmanship. Kira evaluates innovation. Darvish evaluates purpose.

**Quest 11 — "The Vote" (Act III)**
The Compact's internal reckoning: remain independent (Harmon's preference), formalize a faction relationship (Kira's pragmatism), or restructure as a public utility governed by charter (Darvish's quiet suggestion). The player's rank, relationships, and choices determine options and outcomes.

**Quest 12 — "What Holds" (Act III — Resolution)**
The player chooses the Compact's first commission under its new structure — a decision with symbolic and practical weight. Three options: a commission for a **frontier settlement** (the Compact serves the vulnerable first), a commission for **Hollowford's infrastructure** (the Compact builds the center that holds everything together), or a commission for the **player's aligned faction** (the Compact acknowledges political reality). The player then visits the south wall from Quest 1 — repaired, reinforced, maintained throughout the game. The city has not fallen. Harmon stands with the player. What he eventually says depends on everything that came before.

**Central Moral Question:** *Does neutrality become complicity when the world is at war? Can craft transcend politics, or is that a luxury of peacetime?*

# SECTION 3: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## 3.1 Membership Rules

**One order at a time.** This is a meaningful identity commitment.

**Leaving an order:** Quest completion history is preserved. Rank and mechanical benefits are suspended. The player returns to Rank 1 status if they rejoin.

**Rejoining (Reaffirmation):** Completed quests do not need to be replayed. The returning player must meet current skill gates for each rank and complete a Reaffirmation quest — a single narrative-appropriate quest where the order evaluates whether the player is serious about returning. Rank advancement after Reaffirmation is accelerated: skill gates are checked sequentially and ranks are awarded in rapid succession until the player hits a gate they don't meet or reaches their previous rank. A player who has grown in skill since leaving may rank up faster on return than they did originally.

**No order is required.** The game is complete without joining any. Orders are opt-in enrichment.

**Joining is available from Act I.** Entry quests become available after the player completes their origin sequence and arrives in Hollowford. No minimum level, but skill gates at entry (INT 10, SUR/PER 10, or crafting Tier 2) ensure baseline affinity.

**Order quests pace with acts.** Quests 1–4 available in Act I. Quests 5–8 unlock in Act II. Quests 9–12 unlock in Act III.

## 3.2 Skill-Gating Philosophy

Every rank above entry requires both quest completion AND demonstrated skill investment.

The skill requirements are achievable by a player actively playing the relevant archetype but not through casual dabbling. A combat-focused player who never touches crafting cannot reach Artisan rank in the Anvil Compact. A player who never uses magic cannot reach Scholar rank in the Athenaeum. The order recognizes what you actually do.

Skill gates are checked at the moment of **quest turn-in**, not quest acceptance. A player who starts Quest 6 at Runecarving Tier 2 can complete the quest and then level their Runecarving to Tier 3 before turning it in. This prevents frustrating lockouts while maintaining the gate's integrity.

## 3.3 Order Standing and Failure States

Each order has an internal **Standing** value tracking the player's professional conduct. Standing manifests through NPC behavior, dialogue tone, and institutional consequences.

### Standing States

| State | How It Feels | Mechanical Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Commended** | Order NPCs speak warmly. Leadership volunteers information. Ambient dialogue references the player's contributions. | Rank promotion requires only skill gates (no additional conditions). Order merchant prices at 10% discount. |
| **Neutral** | Default state. Professional courtesy. Standard interactions. | Standard advancement. Standard prices. |
| **Cautioned** | **Mandatory scene triggers on next headquarters visit** (see Standing Notification below). Leadership is direct and formal. | Next rank promotion requires a Remedial Quest addressing the specific violation. |
| **Probation** | A senior order member shadows the player on their next quest. This NPC provides commentary and reports to leadership. | Two successive Cautioned events without remediation. |
| **Expelled** | Player removed from the order. All mechanical benefits suspended. Headquarters access revoked. | Three Cautioned events without remediation, or one severe violation. |

### Standing Notification

When standing shifts to **Cautioned**, a **mandatory scene** triggers on the player's next visit to the order headquarters. This is not a UI popup — it is a scripted, unmissable conversation:

- **Athenaeum:** Thessaly intercepts the player in the main hall. "A word. In private." She takes the player to her office and addresses the specific violation directly, explains why it matters to the institution, and assigns the Remedial Quest.
- **Wardens:** Brenna calls the player into the Reach House command room. She does not sit. "What happened at [location]. Tell me your side." She listens, then assigns the Remedial Quest regardless of the explanation — the process is the process.
- **Compact:** Harmon stops the player at their workstation. "Put down your tools." He walks the player to the materials library, pulls the Compact charter from the shelf, and reads the specific standard the player violated. Then assigns the Remedial Quest.

These scenes are authored, voice-acted, and unambiguous. The player knows exactly what happened, why, and what they need to do about it.

### Standing Recovery

| From | To | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Cautioned → Neutral | Complete the assigned Remedial Quest (one quest, order-specific, addresses the violation) |
| Neutral → Commended | Complete one subsequent quest without any standing violations |
| Probation → Neutral | Complete the shadowed quest without violations. The shadow NPC reports favorably. |
| Expelled → Neutral | Complete a **two-quest reinstatement chain**: Quest 1 demonstrates understanding of the violation (order-specific, authored). Quest 2 demonstrates changed behavior in practice (field assignment, observed). Standing returns to Neutral, not Commended. |

### Cautioned Triggers (per order)

**Athenaeum:**
- Sharing research data with faction operatives without Thessaly's authorization
- Using lattice knowledge for destructive purposes witnessed by order members
- Damaging the micro-fracture through reckless experimentation
- Selling Athenaeum texts or research notes

**Wardens:**
- A hunt resulting in civilian casualties through negligence (not unavoidable circumstance — the system distinguishes)
- Killing a creature the order specifically wanted studied alive
- Taking a trophy from a hunt the order considers ecologically significant
- Abandoning a contracted hunt without notification

**Anvil Compact:**
- Selling substandard work under the Compact certification mark
- Using Compact materials for personal profit without authorization
- Field repairs that fail due to documented corner-cutting (the order investigates failures)
- Counterfeiting or allowing counterfeit of the Compact mark

**Design constraint:** Cautioned events are avoidable by any player genuinely engaging with the order's values. They are consequences for acting against the institution you chose to join, not gotcha traps. A player who is expelled should feel they earned it.

### Standing System Fallback (Scope Contingency)

**Full implementation (target):** Five states, specific triggers, mandatory notification scenes, remedial quests, probation shadow NPC, expulsion and reinstatement chain.

**Simplified implementation (fallback if scope requires):** Two states only — Good Standing and Cautioned. Cautioned triggers from the same events but produces only the mandatory notification scene and a single remedial quest. No probation state. No expulsion. No reinstatement chain. The system still gives orders teeth and the player still experiences consequences, but the implementation footprint is approximately 60% smaller. Probation and expulsion can be added in Version 1.1 without breaking anything.

## 3.4 Ambient Faction Friction

Order NPCs produce reactive ambient dialogue when the player's political alignment creates tension with the order's interests. This mirrors the companion approval/disapproval system in Doc 4: triggered by observation, manifested through dialogue, never producing mechanical penalties.

### Triggering

Friction lines activate at **two thresholds** per faction:

**Tier 2 faction standing — Observational.** The NPC registers awareness without direct confrontation. Subtle, deniable, establishing the baseline that the order notices who the player's friends are.

**Tier 3+ faction standing — Confrontational.** The NPC addresses the tension directly. Still not hostile — these are professional colleagues, not enemies. But the discomfort is no longer subtext.

Lines are weighted to trigger once on first visit after the threshold is crossed, then at 20% chance per subsequent visit. Each order has 2–3 Tier 2 lines and 3–5 Tier 3+ lines per major faction.

### Friction Line Examples

**Athenaeum — Tier 2:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 2: Thessaly glances at the Vanguard-issued checkpoint pass visible on the player's desk. Returns to her work without comment.
- Player at Hollow Crown Tier 2: Tomas looks up when the player enters, then down. Reorganizes papers that were already organized.

**Athenaeum — Tier 3+:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 3+: Lyris, passing in the hall: "Our newest Scholar has friends who'd shut us down. Interesting career choice."
- Player at Hollow Crown Tier 3+: Tomas, nervously: "Seraphine's people have been asking about you. And about us. The questions are... specific."
- Player at Reaver Tier 3+: Thessaly, privately: "I don't care who you drink with. I care that our lattice fragment supply isn't showing up on Varek's inventory lists."

**Wardens — Tier 2:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 2: Brenna notices mud on the player's boots consistent with the Garrison courtyard. Says nothing. Makes a note.
- Player at Reaver Tier 2: Old Marsh asks casually whether the player's "friends from the city" have any interesting creature specimens to trade.

**Wardens — Tier 3+:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 3+: Fen: "Your Vanguard friends want to 'contain' corruption zones. You know what that means? Walls. Around the problem. While it grows."
- Player at Reaver Tier 3+: Old Marsh: "Your associates in the Undercity are selling corrupted animal parts as exotic goods. Three people have been hospitalized."
- Player at Hollow Crown Tier 3+: Brenna: "The Hollow Crown offered to 'fund' the Wardens last week. I told them we're not for sale. They said everyone is. I'm telling you this so you know what your friends think of us."

**Compact — Tier 2:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 2: Harmon asks casually whether the player's "military friends" have opinions on the Compact's pricing.
- Player at Hollow Crown Tier 2: Kira mentions that someone from the Noble Quarter has been asking about the new alloy. "Just thought you should know."

**Compact — Tier 3+:**
- Player at Vanguard Tier 3+: Harmon: "Fine craftsmanship. Shame it's going to be requisitioned if your Commander decides our output is 'strategic material.'"
- Player at Reaver Tier 3+: Darvish, after a long silence: "The leather I ordered through your Reaver contacts. The tanning process uses corruption byproducts. I can smell it. Don't order from them again."
- Player at Hollow Crown Tier 3+: Kira: "Lady Eltaryn's people want exclusive access to the new alloy. They offered me a title. A title. As if that's what motivates engineers."

**Critical design rule:** Friction lines never tell the player they're wrong. They tell the player that their professional colleagues have noticed, have opinions, and are paying attention.

## 3.5 Social Recognition System

### NPC Awareness Tags

| Tag | Who Has It | Recognition Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **None** | Generic townsfolk, unnamed NPCs, children | Never recognizes order membership. |
| **Low** | Named merchants, innkeepers, common guards, farmers | Recognizes order membership at Rank 3+ through visible markers (Pin, Cord, or Band). One recognition line per visit. |
| **High** | Faction operatives, quest-relevant NPCs, settlement leaders, named military personnel | Recognizes order membership at any rank. Contextually appropriate recognition line on first interaction per visit. |
| **Order** | NPCs within the player's own order | Always aware. Dialogue reflects rank, standing, and relationship. |

### Recognition Triggering

Recognition triggers on **first interaction per visit**, not proximity. A "visit" resets when the player leaves and returns to a settlement or enters a new map cell containing the NPC. Recognition produces **one contextually appropriate line**, then standard dialogue resumes. No repeated recognition within the same visit.

Recognition lines are pooled: **3 unique lines per NPC archetype per rank range** (Rank 1–2, 3–4, 5). The system cycles through the pool with no repetition until all 3 have been used, then resets. A player visiting Grainfell as a Rank 3 Warden hears a unique recognition line their first 3 visits before hearing a repeat.

### Recognition Line Examples (by archetype)

**Frontier Farmer, Warden Rank 3–4:**
1. "That cord's getting heavy. Good. Means the roads are getting safer."
2. "A Hunter, huh? My cousin's boy wanted to join the Wardens. I told him to learn to run first."
3. "Whatever's in the eastern field — it took two sheep. If you're heading that way..."

**Hollowford Guard, Athenaeum Rank 1–2:**
1. "Athenaeum? You're the ones studying the fractures. Just... keep that stuff in your building, yeah?"
2. "Initiate. Don't blow anything up."
3. "Your lot's been quiet lately. That usually means you're about to not be quiet."

**Merchant (any settlement), Compact Rank 3–4:**
1. "Compact mark on that blade. I'll give you fair price — no haggling needed."
2. "An Artisan! If you have time, my scales need recalibrating. I'll pay."
3. "Compact work. Good. I'm tired of selling things that break."

### Recognition Line Volume

| Scope | Lines per archetype | NPC archetypes | Rank ranges | Total per order | Grand total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per order | 3 | 8 | 3 | 72 | **216** |

This fits within the ambient dialogue pipeline specified in the MVP Roadmap (Doc 10).

## 3.6 Relationship to Existing Systems

**Faction System (Doc 5):** No mechanical interaction. Ambient narrative friction exists (Section 3.4) but produces only dialogue.

**Crafting System (Doc 6):** The Anvil Compact provides exclusive recipes within existing crafting disciplines. The Master Forge is a higher-tier version of existing stations. Compact Certification and Masterwork Stamp add new properties to the existing affix system. Field Reforging extends the crafting system into the field.

**Combat System (Doc 7):** Athenaeum Spell Layering and Warden Hunter's Mark integrate into existing skill trees as additional nodes unlocked by order rank. They extend existing trees, not create parallel ones.

**Companion System (Doc 4):** Order NPCs are not companions. They do not travel with the player or have companion-style relationship scores. Companions have ambient dialogue acknowledging order membership: Aelira comments on Athenaeum research. Kael has opinions about Warden independence. Maren finds the Compact's neutrality either admirable or naive. Seraphine has specific dialogue about Tomas Inkwell.

**Economy System (Doc 11):** Compact Certification adds a flat +20% sell value that stacks with merchant relationship bonuses. The Compact trade network provides rare materials at Partner-tier prices.

**Homestead System (Doc 12):** Order membership unlocks specific homestead buildings: Athenaeum members build a **Lattice Study** (micro-fracture research station, reduced scale). Wardens build a **Trophy Hall** (trophy displays with passive bonuses, bestiary archive). Compact members build a **Master Workshop** (Master Forge equivalent at the homestead with Field Reforging station).

## 3.7 Narrative Intersection Points

Each order's questline intersects with the main story at two mandatory points:

**Intersection 1 (Act II):** The order's central discovery generates information relevant to the main quest's Act II revelation about the Aethercrown Lattice. Order members receive this through the order's lens. Non-members receive it through faction channels. Same data, different framing.

**Intersection 2 (Act III):** The player's aligned faction pressures the order. The player must decide whether professional loyalty or political loyalty takes priority.

# SECTION 5: POST-QUESTLINE CONTENT

## 5.1 Repeatable Content

After the 12-quest questline, each order provides ongoing content.

### Athenaeum — Lattice Anomaly Investigations

Anomalies are drawn from a pool of **12 authored anomaly types with 3 severity variants each** (36 total scenarios). One appears every 5–7 in-game days. Severity determines reward tier:

| Severity | Combat? | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | No | 1 research token + codex fragment |
| Moderate | Optional (hostile elements present) | 2 research tokens + minor lattice-infused item |
| Major | Yes (corrupted guardian or anomaly defense) | 4 research tokens + rare crafting material + codex entry |

Research tokens have a **fixed exchange rate**: 5 tokens = 1 exclusive recipe. 10 tokens = 1 rare lattice material. The player always knows what they're working toward.

### Wardens — Weekly Hunts

A new creature threat reported every **7 in-game days**. Difficulty scales with the player's level at acceptance, capped at player level +2. Hunts cycle through three tiers in sequence:

| Tier | Frequency | Reward | Narrative Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | Every first cycle | Creature components + bestiary fragment | Generic threat report |
| Challenging | Every second cycle | Standard + unique crafting material | Named NPC reporter with context |
| Exceptional | Every third cycle | Challenging + rare drop + story beat | **Authored mini-narrative** drawn from a pool of 8–10 scenarios with unique creature names, backstory, and combat behavior |

Exceptional hunt unique materials are on a **30-day cooldown** before the same material reappears.

### Anvil Compact — Rotating Commissions

Commissions drawn from a pool of **15–20 authored site-and-problem combinations**, appearing every 7–10 in-game days. Each specifies a site, a problem, and material constraints. Difficulty scales with constraint count:

| Constraints Active | Example | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site only (standard materials, no time pressure) | Gold + 1 trade token |
| 2 | Site + material restriction (e.g., no steel available) | Gold + 2 trade tokens |
| 3 | Site + material restriction + time pressure OR environmental hazard | Gold + 3 trade tokens |
| 4 | All constraints active | Gold + 4 trade tokens + **Clean Execution bonus** (50% additional tokens if completed without failures) |

Trade tokens exchange at fixed rates: 5 tokens = rare material. 10 tokens = exclusive recipe.

## 5.2 Order Influence on Endings

Order membership does not create new ending permutations. It **textures** existing faction endings with an additional epilogue modifier — 2–3 sentences acknowledging what the player built professionally.

**Athenaeum epilogue modifiers (examples):**
- Independent: "The Fractured Hall endures. Thessaly Crane's students continue her work, guided by the principles the [Archon title] helped define."
- Subordinated: "The Athenaeum's research continues under [faction] oversight. Some scholars call it progress. Others call it the end of independent inquiry."
- Micro-fracture sealed: "The sealed chamber is quiet now. What was learned there is preserved in the Archon's codex — dangerous knowledge, carefully kept."

**Warden epilogue modifiers (examples):**
- Construct destroyed: "The Wardens' Great Hunt passed into legend. Brenna Ashmark raised a glass to the fallen."
- Construct captured: "In the Reach House basement, a Firstborn machine hums in its cage. The Wardens study it daily."
- Brenna passed leadership: "The Wardens follow a new captain now. Brenna walks the northern road alone, unburdened for the first time in years."

**Compact epilogue modifiers (examples):**
- Independent: "The Maker's Band is still recognized in every market. Harmon Slade smiles more than he used to, though he'd deny it."
- Faction relationship: "The Crucible's forges burn for [faction] now. Harmon retired the day the contract was signed."
- Public utility: "The Compact belongs to everyone now. Darvish's suggestion reshaped an institution."
- First commission choice reflected: The frontier settlement / Hollowford / faction commission the player chose in Quest 12 is referenced as the Compact's defining act under its new structure.

## 6.2 Implementation Priority

Based on the MVP Roadmap (Doc 10), the order system should be implemented in **Phase 3 (Full Production, Months 16–24)**.

**Priority 1 — Ship with base game:**
- Athenaeum of the Fracture (12 quests, full system)
- Wardens of the Reach (12 quests, full system)

- Visual identity markers (3 orders × 5 rank variants)

**Priority 2 — Ship with base game if scope allows; defer to Version 1.1 if not:**
- Anvil Compact (12 quests, full system). If deferred, the Crucible building exists in Hollowford's Forge District as an explorable location with placeholder NPCs — establishing presence before content is available.

**Priority 3 — Version 1.1:**
- Repeatable content (all three orders)
- Probation and expulsion mechanics (if standing system shipped in simplified form)

## 6.3 Risk Assessment

**Highest-risk addition: the Standing system.** Trigger detection, remedial quest authoring, probation shadow NPC behavior, and expulsion/reinstatement chains represent a system-level feature requiring dedicated design and QA time. If shipped half-implemented (triggers fire inconsistently, remedial quests feel generic, expulsion feels arbitrary), it actively harms the player experience more than if it didn't exist.

**Fallback plan:** Ship with two states only (Good Standing and Cautioned). Cautioned triggers from the same events, produces the mandatory notification scene and a single remedial quest. No probation. No expulsion. No reinstatement chain. Implementation footprint ~60% smaller. Probation and expulsion added in Version 1.1.

**Second-highest risk: quest volume.** 36 additional quests brings the game total from ~81 to ~117. This is proportionate for a single playthrough (a player who joins one order has 93 quests, not 117), but the authoring, scripting, voice recording, and QA burden is real. If the Compact is deferred, launch quest count is 105 — still substantial but more manageable.

## 6.4 Total Quest Impact

| Without Orders | With 2 Orders (launch minimum) | With 3 Orders (full) |
|---|---|---|
| ~81 quests | ~105 quests | ~117 quests |
| 35,000–50,000 voiced lines | +1,500 quest lines, +216 recognition, +75 friction = ~1,800 additional | +2,550 additional (all three orders) |

Order quests are parallel content enriching the game without extending the critical path. A player who joins no order still has 81 quests.
