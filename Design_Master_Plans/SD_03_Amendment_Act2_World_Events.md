# SECTION 4.5: THE WORLD IN MOTION

## Design Mandate

Act II covers hours 15–40. For 25 hours, the player is self-directed across faction questlines, companion arcs, dungeon content, and optional systems. This is the correct design. It is also the window where players are most at risk of losing the sense that the *world* is in crisis — not just the dungeons, not just the faction conflicts, but the Vale itself, right now, getting worse.

The events in this section are designed to prevent that drift. They are **mandatory world events** — they fire on timers, not on player triggers. The player cannot cause them. The player cannot prevent them. They happen to the world, whether the player is paying attention or not.

**Design rules for all events in this section:**
1. The player can *respond* but never *prevent*. The event fires. Its outcome may be influenced; its occurrence cannot.
2. Each event has a three-state progression: **Warning** (player hears about it), **Crisis** (it's visibly happening), and **Settled** (it resolved — well or badly, but done).
3. No event requires the player to be anywhere specific. They interrupt or inform; they do not gate.
4. Events should never all resolve in the same direction. Some should be improvable with player intervention. Some should be unimprovable — they are what they are.
5. At least one event should implicate each of the four factions before Act III begins.

## EVENT 01 — THE GRAINFELL BLIGHT
**Timer:** Hours 18–27
**Implicated faction:** Verdant Flame (primary), Vanguard (secondary)
**Stakes:** Grainfell's eastern district, one eastern trade route, 40+ named NPCs

**Warning State (Hour 18):**
The player can observe or hear about it through any of three channels:
- Traveling the Hollowford-Grainfell road: a faint dark discoloration in the grain fields is visible from the road. No NPC commentary unless the player approaches.
- Grainfell's notice board: a hand-written notice from a farmer named Aldene requesting "Verdant Flame assessment — something wrong with the east fields."
- Aelira (if companion): comments while passing near Grainfell: *"The soil here is wrong. Not corrupted — not yet. But something beneath it is sick."*

**Crisis State (Hour 22):**
Two Grainfell farmhands found catatonic in the eastern fields — alive but unresponsive, eyes open, pupils fixed. Vanguard has issued a local alert. The Verdant Flame has sent a field researcher named Brother Fen, who is visibly out of his depth. A visible black vein now extends approximately 80m through the eastern grain rows.

The Verdant Flame side quest "The Failing Crop" (referenced in SD_08 §10) opens here. If the player engages and completes it before Hour 27, the spread is contained. Brother Fen stabilizes. The catatonic farmers recover partially.

**Settled State (Hour 27 — if player did not intervene):**
The eastern district of Grainfell is abandoned. Vanguard has posted a quarantine marker at the field boundary. The Verdant Flame researcher Brother Fen is missing — last seen entering the field alone. Two families displaced from the eastern farms arrive in Hollowford's refugee quarter. Talking to them provides a firsthand account of what the blight felt like from inside.

**World consequences:**
- East Grainfell trade route disrupted (crop output reduced for Acts II-III if not intervened)
- Brother Fen: alive and grateful (intervened) or missing, found later in a small ruin nearby, disoriented but recoverable through a separate side quest (not intervened)
- Visible world change: eastern Grainfell shows visual corruption markers in Act III regardless of intervention — the spread was stopped, not healed

## EVENT 02 — THE VANGUARD CHECKPOINT ESCALATION
**Timer:** Hours 20–25
**Implicated faction:** Vanguard (primary), all others (secondary)
**Stakes:** Freedom of movement throughout the Vale, every faction's surface operations

**Warning State (Hour 20):**
Rumors in Hollowford: Commander Hale has ordered two new checkpoints on the primary roads between Hollowford and the frontier settlements. The official reason: "unauthorized movement of lattice-adjacent materials." The actual reason: Hale is responding to Reaver relic smuggling and wants to intercept lattice chip shipments before they leave his zone of control.

**Crisis State (Hours 21-25):**
The checkpoints are physically present. Every player traveling the main roads encounters them. The checkpoints do not stop the player outright — they create friction:
- Low Vanguard reputation: mandatory search (brief delay, inventory spot-check, minor humiliation)
- Moderate reputation: pass-through with documentation review (can be bypassed with a Vanguard contact)
- High reputation: waved through with a nod

More importantly: every NPC faction is now operating under increased scrutiny. Verdant Flame caravans are delayed. Reaver surface operations become more dangerous. Hollow Crown couriers are being intercepted. The player observes this whether they interact with the checkpoints or not.

**Settled State (Hour 25 onward):**
The checkpoints become permanent features of the Vale until Act III, where they are resolved by the player's faction alignment. They're not a crisis — they're a new normal. A new normal that makes every other faction angrier.

**World consequences:**
- Trade route delays for Verdant Flame and Reaver-aligned merchants (economic impact on established routes)
- Kael (if companion) grows visibly uncomfortable: *"These orders are Hale's. I didn't know he was doing this while I was in the field with you. I don't know what to do with that."* This is a small forward beat on his Act II break.
- Players who bypassed the checkpoints through Reaver assistance gain minor Reaver Fame and minor Vanguard Infamy regardless of other reputation — the checkpoints are watching

## EVENT 03 — THE SOUTHWATCH FEVER
**Timer:** Hours 23–32
**Implicated faction:** None (humanitarian crisis, no faction responds adequately)
**Stakes:** Southwatch settlement, Drakeling refugee population, two minor NPCs the player met in Act I

**Warning State (Hour 23):**
A traveler arriving in Hollowford from the south mentions that Southwatch's refugee camp has "a sickness going through it — the Drakeling camp, mostly." No further detail available without traveling there.

**Crisis State (Hours 25-30):**
A fever is spreading through Southwatch's Drakeling community. The Vanguard medic assigned to Southwatch (an NPC named Corporal Yann) is overwhelmed and has requested supply support — the request is buried in Hale's queue. The Verdant Flame has herbal supplies but no Southwatch presence. The Reavers are aware but not engaged (no profit in it).

If the player has visited Southwatch in Act I and met Pyra Flameheart (the Drakeling leader NPC), they receive a direct message from her through a courier: *"People are dying. Not quickly. Slowly. The kind of dying that means something went wrong weeks ago and we didn't notice until now."*

The player can:
- Bring Verdant Flame herbal supplies to Southwatch (requires Verdant Flame reputation or purchase from their Hollowford contact)
- Escort Corporal Yann's supply request through Hale's queue using Vanguard reputation or political leverage
- Pay for private medical supplies from the Hollowford black market (Reaver Favor Markers accepted, 20 Crown Marks alternative)
- Do nothing

**Settled State (Hour 32 — if no intervention):**
Twelve Drakelings die. Pyra Flameheart's dialogue changes permanently — she is harder, more closed, less willing to engage with the player or any faction. The Southwatch refugee camp's population is visibly reduced. A small grave marker area appears outside the camp. No faction acknowledges it publicly.

**World consequences:**
- Pyra Flameheart relationship: +20 if intervened, -15 if not (affects Drakeling expansion content readiness)
- Corporal Yann: alive and grateful if intervened, transferred out of Southwatch by an angry superior if not (the "failure" was visible to Hale, who removes him to avoid embarrassment)
- The event is never referenced in any official faction communiqué. The only acknowledgment is from the Drakelings themselves.

## EVENT 04 — THE RELIC FIRE AT ASHFORD
**Timer:** Hours 28–34
**Implicated faction:** Ashmar Reavers (primary), Hollow Crown (secondary)
**Stakes:** Ashford settlement, one major trade warehouse, a Hollow Crown archive cache

**Warning State (Hour 28):**
A fire breaks out in Ashford's commercial district. Initial reports: a relic storage facility owned by a Reaver-adjacent merchant caught fire during a "routine assessment." The Hollow Crown's local agent is making noise about "illegal relic handling."

**Crisis State (Hours 29-34):**
The fire is out — the physical crisis passed quickly. The political crisis is just beginning. The warehouse contained a Hollow Crown archive cache that was stored there in secret (Hollow Crown was using Reaver storage under a false merchant identity to move documents without Vanguard scrutiny). The fire destroyed most of the cache — but not all of it. Some documents survived. Someone has them.

The Reaver faction is claiming ignorance. The Hollow Crown is claiming theft. The Vanguard wants to know why the Hollow Crown was operating a secret archive in Reaver-adjacent property.

The player can investigate (connects to the Hollow Crown faction questline, unlocks specific documents that provide early access to Seraphine's family history), broker a resolution between factions (requires high standing with at least two factions), or ignore it (the documents are quietly recovered by Reaver operatives who sell them to the highest bidder in Act III).

**Settled State (Hour 34 onward — if not investigated):**
The documents surface in Act III as leverage in the "The Crown Falls" mission (if Seraphine is antagonist). If investigated and given to Seraphine, they appear in "The Companion in the Dark" with different dialogue — she already knows what they say. This is not a major plot change but a texture difference that attentive players will notice.

**World consequences:**
- Ashford's commercial district has fire damage markers through Act III
- One Ashford merchant NPC (Harren, the feed trader referenced in SD_08) has lost a storage contract and complains about it for the rest of the game
- Minor Hollow Crown reputation change based on player's handling of the documents

## EVENT 05 — THE IRON MARKET COLLAPSE
**Timer:** Hours 30–38
**Implicated faction:** All four factions (economic)
**Stakes:** Crown Mark purchasing power, all merchant pricing, Hollow Crown political standing

**Warning State (Hour 30):**
A Hollowford merchant named Seli Cord (Market Quarter, established Act I contact) mentions that iron prices "have doubled in the last week." She doesn't know why. *"Someone's hoarding, or someone's cutting supply, or both. Nothing moves in this town without someone profiting from the friction."*

**Crisis State (Hours 32-36):**
Iron has effectively stopped flowing through standard Hollowford markets. The Crown Mint has attempted to compensate by issuing new Crown Marks — but without iron-backed production to support them, the new marks are promissory notes for goods that don't exist. Crown Mark purchasing power drops 8% across all markets. Prices rise.

The cause: the Vanguard requisitioned all Highwinter iron shipments for fortification projects. The Hollow Crown is intercepting caravan routes to redirect iron to their own strongholds. The Reavers are buying and holding iron as a speculative asset. No one faction is entirely responsible; all four are contributing.

The player observes this through merchant dialogue (prices change in the UI — not dramatically, but visibly), NPC complaints, and faction communication interception available through the Reaver network or Hollow Crown contacts.

If the player has established trade routes (SD_11), two caravan masters notify them directly: iron-dependent shipments are delayed or rerouted.

**Settled State (Hour 38 onward):**
Iron supply stabilizes at a new higher equilibrium — prices don't return to previous levels, but the dramatic rise stops. This becomes the new baseline. Crown Mark devaluation is permanent for this playthrough.

**World consequences:**
- All iron-based crafting recipes cost 15% more Crown Marks through Act III
- The Hollow Crown's political standing in Hollowford's Market Quarter declines (merchants blame them for the shortage, correctly)
- If the player intervened by using economic leverage to break the Vanguard requisition or Reaver hold, iron prices stabilize faster and the Crown Mark devaluation is reduced to 5%

## EVENT 06 — THE ECHO'S SILENCE
**Timer:** Hour 33 — single event, not a progression
**Implicated faction:** None (personal/narrative)
**Stakes:** The player's relationship with the Echo; Act II emotional pacing

**The Event:**
For approximately 90 minutes of gameplay (real time, not game time), the Echo goes completely silent. No ambient observations. No responses to player prompts. No reaction to corruption zones or Firstborn ruins.

This happens after the Echo has revealed enough of Erevos's identity to understand what it is — and before it has processed the implications. It is the Echo's equivalent of going into a back room and not coming out.

There is no warning. The Echo simply stops responding.

During this period:
- Players who have grown accustomed to the Echo's presence notice the absence
- Environments that previously triggered Echo reactions (ruins, corruption zones) feel emptier
- NPC conversations continue normally — but dialogue trees that used to offer an option to share what the Echo said now don't have that branch

After approximately 90 minutes (triggered by the player entering a specific location — a ruin, a Wellspring shrine, or beginning a companion quest — whichever comes first), the Echo returns:

*"I needed... time. I heard what Erevos said. Through the relay. I heard myself, from the outside. I needed to understand what that meant. I think I do now. I think I'm ready."*

**World consequences:**
- No mechanical effect
- Players who missed the silence entirely (logged off, spent time in menus, rushed through content) will not notice
- Players who were in heavy dialogue during the silence period may not notice
- Players who were exploring, traveling, or doing companion content will feel it — and will remember feeling it

This event has no state flag, no journal entry, and no reward. It is entirely atmospheric. It is included because *the Echo going quiet for an hour should be one of the most unsettling things that happens in Act II*, and the unsettling quality of it should be discovered, not announced.

## EVENT 07 — THE LATTICE NODE FAILURE AT IRONWOOD
**Timer:** Hours 35–40
**Implicated faction:** Verdant Flame (primary), Hollow Crown (secondary)
**Stakes:** Ironwood settlement stability, northern Vale corruption zone expansion

**Warning State (Hour 35):**
Verdant Flame monitoring crystals in the northern Vale begin reporting anomalous readings. Aelira (if companion) receives a message from Elder Thorn: *"The Ironwood node is fluctuating. It's not catastrophic — yet. But the rate of change is accelerating."*

**Crisis State (Hours 37-39):**
The Ironwood lattice node partially fails. The corruption zone in the northwestern Vale expands by approximately 200m overnight — visible from the Ironwood settlement as a creeping line of dark growth moving toward the treeline. The Ironwood residents (mostly logging families and a Hollow Crown-adjacent timber operation) are not in immediate danger, but the trajectory is clear.

The Verdant Flame attempts an emergency monitoring operation. The Hollow Crown's Ironwood contact (referenced in SD_08) attempts to evacuate the timber operation's records — not the people, the records.

The player can:
- Assist the Verdant Flame monitoring operation (arrests the node's deterioration rate, buys months)
- Help evacuate Ironwood civilians (the Hollow Crown contact is focused on records — someone has to focus on people)
- Use the relic to interface directly with the failing node (high risk, high reward — potential to stabilize the node permanently at cost of several HP and a temporary debuff)
- Do nothing

**Settled State (Hour 40 — Act II transition approaching):**
The node stabilizes at a reduced capacity — the expansion stops, but the corruption zone's gains are permanent. Ironwood is now a settlement visibly close to a corruption boundary. This becomes a visual and mechanical feature of Act III's Ironwood (the corruption zone is closer, some Ironwood locations are now in the zone's ambient effect radius).

**World consequences:**
- Ironwood corruption boundary visible in Act III
- The Hollow Crown contact's behavior during the crisis (focusing on records over people) becomes a conversation topic with Seraphine — she is embarrassed by it if brought up
- Successful relic interface earns a small permanent bonus to relic ability effectiveness (+3% relic effectiveness) — the node, even failing, recognizes the Firstborn interface

## Pacing Summary

| Event | Window | Primary Faction | Resolvable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Grainfell Blight | Hours 18–27 | Verdant Flame | Yes |
| 02 — Checkpoint Escalation | Hours 20–25 | Vanguard | No (becomes permanent) |
| 03 — Southwatch Fever | Hours 23–32 | None | Yes |
| 04 — Relic Fire at Ashford | Hours 28–34 | Reavers / Hollow Crown | Partially |
| 05 — Iron Market Collapse | Hours 30–38 | All four | Partially |
| 06 — The Echo's Silence | Hour 33 | None (personal) | N/A |
| 07 — Node Failure at Ironwood | Hours 35–40 | Verdant Flame / Hollow Crown | Partially |

**Gap analysis:** The window between Hours 15-18 (Act II opening) is intentionally event-free — the player needs settling time after the Act I climax. The window Hours 38-40 is intentionally light — the Ashenveil Archive is approaching and pacing should lean toward that convergence.
