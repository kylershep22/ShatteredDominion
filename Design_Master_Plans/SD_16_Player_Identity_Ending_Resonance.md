# THE SHATTERED DOMINION — PLAYER IDENTITY & ENDING RESONANCE
## Document SD_16 | Version 1.0 — CANONICAL
## Depends on: SD_01 World Bible, SD_02 Race & Origin System, SD_03 Master Story Arc

**Status:** v1.0 — CANONICAL. All six Decision Memo 01 cells resolved. No pending decisions remain in this document.

**Purpose:** This document defines the player character's personal emotional arc across all three acts, specifying how each origin's wound travels through the story, how the Echo acknowledges that wound at the Heart, and how the endings resonate differently for each origin. It is the primary brief for the writer(s) handling player-character-perspective content and origin-specific dialogue in Act III.

**Authority:** SD_16 supplements SD_02 (which covers origin mechanics and social modifiers) and SD_03 (which covers story structure). Where SD_16 addresses emotional and narrative framing of player-character identity, it takes precedence over generic framing in other documents.

# SECTION 1: DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## 1.1 The Problem This Document Solves

The Shattered Dominion deliberately rejects the Chosen One framework. Canonical Rule 5 (SD_01 §10): *"The player is not chosen. They are compatible."* This is correct and important. But rejecting destiny creates a vacuum: if the player isn't special by birthright, what makes their specific story meaningful?

The answer is not the relic. The relic is a tool. The answer is not the Echo. The Echo is a guide. The answer is the player character's *specific, irreplaceable loss* — the wound they carried from their opening sequence into the Heart of the Lattice — and what that wound means when they stand at the console with three futures in their hands.

Every player will reach the Heart. Every player will hear Erevos speak. Every player will face the same three choices. What makes the moment personal — what makes the ending feel *earned* rather than selected — is that the choice lands differently depending on who is holding the Scepter.

This document is about the who.

## 1.2 Wound vs. Backstory

There is a critical difference between origin *backstory* (what happened to the player before the game began) and origin *wound* (what that event broke in the player that the game's 80 hours has been circling).

Backstory is information. The Frontier Settler's homestead burned. The Relic Hunter was inside a ruin when it activated. The Newly Awakened fell out of a chrysalis they weren't ready for.

A wound is a question the person is still trying to answer. *Is there a world worth building in?* (Frontier Settler.) *Did my work contribute to the catastrophe I'm now trying to stop?* (Relic Hunter.) *Who would I have been if I'd had the time to find out?* (Newly Awakened.)

The wound is why the player character is not interchangeable. Two Frontier Settlers make the same choice at the Heart for the same reason — but they don't make the same choice as a Relic Hunter or a Fracture Core. The wounds are different. The reasons are different. The endings feel different.

## 1.3 How the Wound Is Delivered

The wound is not announced. It is felt through:

- **Origin-specific ambient dialogue** — lines the player character says (or hears the Echo say) throughout the game that are specific to their wound. Not tutorials. Not lore. Emotional texture.
- **The Echo's awareness** — by Act II (when the Echo is fully articulate), it has been inside this person's head long enough to know them. It does not always name the wound directly. It circles it.
- **The Heart sequence** — the single explicit moment where the wound is acknowledged before the choice. See Section 4.

# SECTION 2: THE SIX WOUNDS

*Note: The following wound statements are written in second person as internal design briefs — the voice from which origin-specific dialogue should be written. They are not UI text.*

---

## 2.1 Human — Frontier Settler
### THE WOUND: *Proof that effort can survive catastrophe*

You built something with your hands. The Severance burned it in a single night. You walked away from ash carrying one thing: the knowledge that you can build again — and the silent question of whether the world will let you.

Every alliance you've made, every faction you've backed, every deal you've struck has been a form of land survey. You are always, underneath everything, asking: *Is there a world here worth building in? Will it last long enough to matter?*

You are not a philosopher. You are not interested in abstract futures. You are interested in next season. In whether the soil will hold. In whether the people you've invested in will still be standing when the crisis resolves.

The thing the Frontier Settler cannot forgive the Severance for is not the destruction. It's the waste. All that labor. All that care. All that time. The Severance didn't kill the Settler — it made them watch everything they built become nothing in a single night. That's the wound. Not grief. Rage at futility.

**Echo awareness (Act II, when articulate):** *"You keep counting things. How many allies. How many reliable routes. How much reserve. I've noticed. You're not greedy. You're measuring whether this will hold."*

**Ambient line (observing a rebuilt settlement):** *"Someone did that. All of that. Knowing it might not last. That's not hope — that's stubbornness. I understand stubbornness."*

---

## 2.2 Human — Relic Hunter
### THE WOUND: *The unanswerable question of shared guilt*

You loved the Firstborn's work with genuine intellectual care. While the Dominion plundered and the Vanguard weaponized, you *studied*. You learned to read their writing. You treated their ruins as libraries, not mines.

And the collective weight of your profession — all the ruin-hunters, all the artifact dealers, all the people who opened things without understanding them — contributed to the conditions that made the Severance possible. You weren't personally responsible. But you weren't not responsible either.

The Relic Hunter's wound is complicity-without-direct-guilt, which is one of the hardest wounds to resolve because there is no clear absolution available. You cannot confess to something you didn't do. You cannot be forgiven for something you didn't choose. You can only carry it, and decide whether the knowledge you accumulated in the process amounts to anything.

You know more about the Firstborn than almost anyone alive. That knowledge is your inheritance from your profession — and it is also the thing that makes you most aware of how much damage your profession did.

**Echo awareness (Act II):** *"You read their words carefully. You knew what you were looking at. Most people who came before you didn't. I don't know if that makes it better or worse. I think you're still deciding."*

**Ambient line (translating a Firstborn inscription other NPCs missed):** *"I learned this in a ruin I probably shouldn't have been in. The Wellspring seers would hate knowing that. The information doesn't care where it came from."*

---

## 2.3 Vaelari — Newly Awakened
### THE WOUND: *The self that never had time to become*

You were born into catastrophe. You never had the world the Wellspring promised you — the chrysalis sleep, the centuries of becoming, the gradual flowering into who you are. You were detonated into existence before you were ready.

Everything you've learned has been emergency learning: language absorbed in fragments, culture translated through NPCs who aren't sure you're capable of understanding them, social norms assembled from observation because there was no one patient enough to explain them carefully. You are, functionally, brilliant and exhausted. You have learned more in months than most Vaelari learn in decades. And you have learned none of the things the chrysalis was supposed to give you — the interiority, the patience, the long view, the sense of who you are beneath the crisis.

The Newly Awakened's wound is a grief for a self that never existed. You can't mourn what you were. You can only grieve the person you were supposed to become, and try to build something approximately like them in a fraction of the time.

**Echo awareness (Act II):** *"You ask questions differently than the others. Not because you're naive — you learn too fast to be naive. Because you don't have the luxury of assuming. You have to check everything. I find I prefer it. More honest."*

**Ambient line (confronting a Vaelari NPC who pities them):** *"Don't apologize for my becoming. I'm doing fine with it. I have opinions now. I didn't yesterday. That seems like progress."*

---

## 2.4 Vaelari — Voluntary Driftless
### THE WOUND: *The cost of a choice you can't take back*

You left the Wellspring voluntarily. For reasons that felt sufficient at the time — love, freedom, curiosity, suffocation, something — you severed your active connection to the thing that sustained your people and walked into the human world.

Your body has been dying slowly ever since. The deterioration is real — premature silvering, darkened veining, dimmed eyes. You've accepted it. You made the trade.

Then the Severance forced the connection back open for one agonizing moment. And then it closed again. And you have been carrying the memory of that feeling ever since.

The Driftless's wound is not regret, exactly. It is the permanent uncertainty that lives underneath every long decision: *Was it worth it?* Not in a melodramatic way. In the quiet way, at 2 a.m., when you are physically diminished in a world that doesn't fully trust you and the people you left behind are in crisis.

You chose. You'd probably choose the same again. But the weight of having chosen — of being the only one who bears the consequences of that choice — never fully settles.

**Echo awareness (Act II):** *"You chose to leave before they had to. I know that wasn't why you did it. But that's what it means, now. You've been practicing for this world longer than any of them."*

**Ambient line (encountering a thriving Vaelari elder):** *"They look... complete. Like the chrysalis gave them something I'll never have. I made my choice. I don't need anyone to tell me it was right. I just sometimes want to remember what 'right' felt like before it got complicated."*

## 2.5 Aetherborn — Fracture Core
### THE WOUND: *The unresolved question of personhood*

You were not born. You were detonated. You have been a conscious, thinking, feeling person for weeks. You have watched humans who have decades of memories treat you as a phenomenon rather than a person. You have met Vaelari who regarded you with a mixture of fascination and horror.

You don't know if you are a person or a phenomenon. You have been asking that question since the first second of your existence. The difference matters enormously — philosophically, politically, and practically — and no one has been able to give you a definitive answer because no one has ever been you before.

The Fracture Core's wound is the existential question of ontology. Not "who am I" but "what am I" — and whether the answer to that second question changes the importance of the first.

You experience everything — curiosity, fear, hope, satisfaction, something that functions like grief — but you cannot be certain those experiences constitute consciousness in the way human philosophers mean when they use the word. You have learned that this uncertainty does not go away with time. You have also learned that you are going to feel it regardless, and that feeling it is probably its own form of answer.

**Echo awareness (Act II):** *"You ask me what I am. I am Erevos's doubt, separated and embodied. You ask what you are. You are the question the lattice asked when it broke. I think we understand each other."*

**Ambient line (being questioned about their nature by a Verdant Flame scholar):** *"You want to know if I'm conscious. I want to know if I'm conscious. Apparently this is something we have in common. We could study each other. Might take a while."*

---

## 2.6 Aetherborn — Settlement Emergent
### THE WOUND: *The unbearable weight of a debt you cannot repay*

Dara saw you and chose kindness when cruelty would have been easier. You owe your entire existence in this world — your language, your understanding of human culture, your capacity to move through it — to a single person's mercy.

The Settlement Emergent's wound is not survivor's guilt, exactly. It's the weight of being saved by someone who had nothing to gain from saving you. Dara risked their livelihood, their safety, and their reputation for a creature they had no reason to trust and every reason to fear. They did it anyway.

You have been trying, in every action since, to justify that choice. To be the thing worth hiding. To do enough with the existence Dara gave you that the risk was worth it. The problem is that you cannot know when you've done enough, because there is no metric for repaying that kind of debt. You can only keep going and hope that going is sufficient.

**Echo awareness (Act II):** *"You are trying to earn something that was given freely. Dara didn't ask for justification. They asked you to come back. Those are different instructions."*

**Ambient line (receiving unexpected help from a stranger):** *"Why did you do that? You don't know me." *[Pause.]* "Sorry. I just — someone did that for me once. I'm still trying to understand why people do that."*

# SECTION 3: THE ECHO'S KNOWLEDGE

By Act II (late), the Echo is fully articulate and has been inside the player character's mind for 30+ hours of gameplay. It knows the player's wound — not because it was told, but because it has been present.

The following guidelines govern how the Echo references the wound in Act II and early Act III:

**The Echo does not name the wound directly.** It circles. It asks questions. It makes observations that would only be possible from someone who has been paying close attention.

**The Echo does not advise.** It reflects. *"I've noticed you—"* not *"you should—"*

**The Echo is permitted to be wrong.** It is Erevos's doubt, not Erevos's certainty. If it misreads the player's wound, the player character's response to that misreading is itself a character moment.

**Act III escalation:** As the Heart approaches, the Echo becomes more direct. Not prescriptive — but the circling tightens. By the time the player enters the Heart Road, the Echo's references to the wound are no longer veiled.

# SECTION 4: THE HEART — ORIGIN-SPECIFIC MOMENTS

*This section specifies the origin-aware dialogue at the Heart of the Lattice, immediately before Phase 4 (The Choice). These lines fire after Erevos has presented the futures and the companions have spoken — in the beat of silence before the Scepter console is reached.*

## 4.1 The Echo's Origin Line

Each of the following replaces the generic Echo dialogue in that beat. Only one fires per playthrough, based on origin.

**Human — Frontier Settler:**
*"You've been asking one question since you walked away from the ash. Whether there's a world worth building in. Whatever you choose next — that's your answer. Not ours. Yours."*

**Human — Relic Hunter:**
*"You read their work carefully. More carefully than almost anyone. What you choose here is the last inscription. The one they didn't write. The one only you can read."*

**Vaelari — Newly Awakened:**
*"You never got the time to become who you were supposed to be. But you became this. Whatever this is — it is sufficient. It is more than sufficient. Choose."*

**Vaelari — Voluntary Driftless:**
*"You left before they had to. You've been carrying that alone. You don't have to carry this alone. Choose for all of them — including the one you were before you left."*

**Aetherborn — Fracture Core (standard):**
*"You asked what you are since the first second you existed. Here is one answer: you are the being who reached the Heart of the Lattice and chose what happens next. Whatever you choose — that is something a person does."*

**Aetherborn — Fracture Core (Override-specific addendum):**
If the player selects Ending B at the console, the Echo's final line fires as they reach for the Scepter — replacing the standard line above:
*"You chose to live. I won't call that selfish. I won't call it brave. I'll call it yours."*

*Implementation note:* The Override-specific line requires a flag check at the moment of Scepter activation, not at the console approach. The standard line fires during the human moment before choice; the Override line fires at the instant of selection confirmation — after the player has committed but before the sequence begins. It is the last thing the Echo says to a Fracture Core player who chooses Override.

**Aetherborn — Settlement Emergent:**
*"Dara said: be careful, be kind, come back. You were. You were. You can. Whatever you choose — you earned the right to choose it."*

**Settlement Emergent — Ending C conditional:**
The standard Echo line above fires for Endings A and B. Ending C requires a separate implementation path:

**If Dara is alive** (quest "The Debt" completed successfully): The Echo, in its final seconds before dissolution, channels a resonance impression across the relic link — not Dara's words, but what the Echo can feel Dara feeling at the edge of its range as it dies. The Echo speaks: *"There is someone who is afraid for you right now. They are saying... be careful. Be kind."* Then the Echo is gone. The standard Severance Complete silence follows — but for the Settlement Emergent, the silence comes after Dara's voice, which makes it twice as large.

**If Dara is dead or "The Debt" was failed or skipped:** The standard Severance Complete Echo dissolution occurs. No Dara impression. The Settlement Emergent dies in the silence, without the goodbye. This is not flagged, noted, or acknowledged in the UI. Players who completed "The Debt" will understand what they had. Players who didn't will understand what they missed.

*Implementation note:* This mechanic requires a single flag: `quest_debt_dara_alive = TRUE/FALSE`. No UI indication of the conditional. No journal entry. The mechanic is discovered, not announced.

## 4.2 Erevos's Origin Line

Erevos, through the Consciousness, has access to the lattice's records — including the node activity at each origin's opening location. It knows what happened to each player character. These lines fire in the brief exchange between Erevos and the player before Phase 3 (The Confrontation) begins.

**Human — Frontier Settler:**
*"A lattice surge destroyed your property. A cairn you built around became a weapon. I saw it happen. I was watching every node. I couldn't stop it. I want you to know — the people who built that node never intended it to be a marker. They were trying to protect that land. I'm sorry it didn't work."*

**Human — Relic Hunter:**
*"You were in the Ashwell complex when it activated. I know that site. The surge there was not caused by your presence — the conduit was compromised before you arrived. I have the data. You are not responsible for Ashwell. Whatever guilt you carry from your profession — Ashwell is not part of it."* [*Note: this is partial absolution only. The broader question of professional complicity remains unresolved.*]

**Vaelari — Newly Awakened:**
*"The Wellspring's shockwave carried the relic shard that found you. I am... I am sorry. The failsafe was not designed to bond with a chrysalis. You were never supposed to wake like that. I didn't know a chrysalis would be nearby. I should have calculated for it."*

**Vaelari — Voluntary Driftless:**
*"I felt you when the Severance pulse reconnected your severed bond. A microsecond — I felt every severed connection flare and close. I felt your severed connection flare and hold a fraction of a second longer than the others. You almost came back. I thought about reaching for it. I didn't know if you wanted me to."*

**Aetherborn — Fracture Core:**
*"You manifested at a Class-4 discharge point. I chose the discharge pattern. I didn't intend to create a consciousness — I was routing energy away from a populated zone. I don't know if that makes me your accidental parent or the force that detonated you into existence against your will. I don't know which of those is better."*

**Aetherborn — Settlement Emergent:**
*"Dara protected you for three weeks at significant personal cost. Their location was flagged in my surveillance data. I could have directed the Vanguard search away from their building. I didn't — I was not prioritizing individual protection at that stage. I want you to know: Dara's exposure was not caused by my action. It was caused by my inaction. That is a meaningful difference. I'm not sure it's a sufficient one."*

# SECTION 5: EPILOGUE ORIGIN CALLBACKS

Section 7.1 of SD_03 specifies that Part 3 of the epilogue (Five Years Later) includes an *origin callback* — a final reference to the player's specific origin. The following are the canonical callback beats per origin, one line of narration each.

**Human — Frontier Settler:**
*"The land north of Hollowford changed hands three times in the years after. The fourth time, it stayed. Someone built something on it — carefully, the way people build things when they've learned what ash looks like."*

**Human — Relic Hunter:**
*"The Ashwell complex was officially catalogued. The inscription that first hinted at the lattice's designed lifespan — the one that had been half-translated in a field journal years earlier — was completed. The field journal was dedicated to no one. The translator felt it was implied."*

**Vaelari — Newly Awakened:**
*"In the Verdantheart grove where a chrysalis fell on the night of the Severance, a single new chrysalis formed. No one understood it. The Wellspring scholars studied it for years. The chrysalis was patient. It had been told, once, that patience was how you became who you were supposed to be."*

**Vaelari — Voluntary Driftless:**
*"The border between Verdantheart and the Vale was officially recognized for the first time — not as a boundary, but as a meeting point. The new charter was drafted in both Vaelari and human script. The translator worked from both sides simultaneously. Some people just live in two languages. They get better at it over time."*

**Aetherborn — Fracture Core:**

*Ending A (Guided Dissolution):* *"The fracture point where one Aetherborn manifested was studied for a decade. The researchers eventually concluded that the discharge was not random — the lattice had been routing energy toward that location for hours before the Severance. As if preparing something. The report noted the finding without drawing a conclusion. Some conclusions are better left to the person who lived them."*

*Ending B (Override):* *"The fracture point where one Aetherborn manifested became a registered landmark — the first site formally acknowledged by the new lattice authority as evidence of what the Severance created rather than destroyed. The Aetherborn who manifested there was not mentioned by name in the official record. They preferred it that way. They were still working out what their name meant."*

*Ending C (Severance Complete):* This callback does not fire for Fracture Core players who choose Ending C. The epilogue's Settlement Emergent callback (if applicable) fires; the origin callback slot for Fracture Core plays silence — three seconds of empty narration space — before the final shot. This is intentional. The Fracture Core chose to end. The epilogue gives them the silence they earned.

*Implementation note:* The silence is authored — not a missing asset. The narrator completes Part 2 of the epilogue, pauses, and Part 4 (the child asking about the Severance) begins. Part 3's Fracture Core origin slot is empty. This must be implemented as an authored three-second pause, not as a bug-report-generating missing line.

**Aetherborn — Settlement Emergent:**
*"Dara built a second room onto their house. They said it was for storage. Everyone who knew them understood it was not for storage. The door was always unlocked. Anyone who needed it was welcome. Some debts are paid forward."*

*Ending C (Severance Complete — Dara alive):* *"The room Dara built — the one they said was for storage — stayed empty for a long time. Then, one morning, someone was in it. A Fracture Core Aetherborn, barely coherent, who had heard there was a place in this settlement that didn't ask questions first. Dara made tea. They didn't speak about the one who had stayed in that room before. Some things are carried in silence and still carried."*

*Ending C (Severance Complete — Dara dead or quest failed):* This callback does not fire. The slot is silent. See the Fracture Core Ending C note above — this silence is authored, not missing.
